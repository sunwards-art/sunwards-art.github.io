The tall black trees that extended for miles began to break. <span class="fx-impact">Like bones cracking.</span> The roasted wood dropped parts of itself, falling down like ashes. The noise they produced was loud enough to wake up the travelers who were still sleeping.

Far away—hidden by the woods—Death was moving silently. Her senses were far better than any human could experience. She could see ants move from miles away. In the distance, she observed the camp.

<Silence height="sm" ornament="dot" />

They both woke up and a new day began for them. They quickly started to gather their belongings and put them in the wheelbarrow. They were fast at doing so, for a life time of repeating the same routine made them efficient.

When everything was packed, the journey resumed. They had to cover their faces for breathing was hard with the falling ashes. They used soaked clothes and continued walking with the cracking wood accompanying their steps. The father dragged the wheelbarrow while Elana carried a heavy bag on her shoulders. The floor was hard to walk and the added weight only made it worse. The roots had grown out of the ground, looking for nourishment, and they crossed and twisted with each other. Making the movement a nuisance.

The path that the old man estimated would only take a day suddenly seemed <span class="fx-stretch">endless.</span> Slow was their advance and quickly progressed the day. They would not reach the end of the forest before evening and would have to stop again.

Elana sang to appease the mood. Her unrefined voice didn't matter to her father who listened with a smile in his heart. Mumbles from her covered mouth—but beautiful mumbles for him. He loved to hear her singing and taught her all the songs he knew, he even tried to make his own songs for her—with varying results—and then sing together on the coldest nights. He knew that they were both alive only for they had stayed positive. That's why the old man feared for her daughter, scared that she would lose hope if something happened to him.

*When hope is gone, the body and mind die with it*, he thought. That's exactly what happened to his aunt after she was widowed and the same would’ve happened to him if he didn’t have Elana. Existence was so much better if you could share it with someone. If one were to be completely alone in the world, why even exist?

He feared for her, her mental state and what would she do alone. Cold was a worry, hunger a problem, but <span class="fx-key">loneliness—Loneliness was a curse.</span>

The old man started to cough but he disguised it as mumbling and did as if nothing was happening. He was cold, but his wasn’t as much of a problem, the movement warmed him enough to continue. He dragged the cart, bumping on each root, killing his back. Pain didn’t matter to him, he always continued, always moving forward. He only wanted to see his daughter as happy as their context allowed.

He looked at her as she walked in front of him. He smiled and carried on. That was always enough. Just a smile and the world became brighter.

<MoodShift theme="bone" label="THE SWEET FOREST" />

They had arrived in a more open area where the sky was visible and they could walk easier. This resulted in great relief for them, especially for the old tired man who had to face the irregularities of the ground with the added weight of the cart. The forest started to change progressively the more they walked into it. Bumps hung on the top—a lot of them. The dark trees gained color, some burned leaves were left. Crystalized snow reflecting the small amount of light they got. Purples, blues and pinks. The snow of the trunks shot light to the floor, making it brighter. Suddenly the forest did not seem as gloomy any more. And the daughter sang happily.

The father noticed something familiar on the scene they were appreciating, but he could not remember exactly what. *Bumps hanging from trees*, he thought. Where did he hear of that? He probably knew of the place before—even told Elana about it—but he did not remember. He was the carrier of too many stories—ages of knowledge transmitted orally—all in his head. Now, sadly, his mind had become as slow as his body. He had learned everything he knew from his uncle; he was the one who taught him how to survive the dark days. He traveled all over the world meeting new people and places that didn’t exist anymore. He was amazed by the world before him and wanted to learn as much about it as he could and all this information passed to Elana. He didn’t know how to read—that was lost many generations ago—but all the songs, tales and myths he knew were far more knowledge than any book could contain.

*The Sweet Forest*, he thought. *That’s what my uncle had called it.*

He opened the map. The name was just a bunch of nonsense symbols to his eyes, but he was correct. That was the Sweet Forest, named like that for good reason.

“We'll spend the night here,” said the man breaking Elana’s song. “I have something to check.”

Elana nodded with intrigue and began to get some water. The father set the cart aside and looked around. Sword in hand, he walked a few meters away, cutting the dead weeds full of thorns around the shiny trees. Once he reached the trunk, a bulge was exposed hanging from one of the lower branches. He let out a laugh that caught Elana’s attention.

<Silence height="sm" ornament="dot" />

"You're going to like this,” the man said.

“And what would that be?” Elana asked. She was already melting some snow.

“Quick, come over here that I need your help.”

The girl left the shovel on the ground and came to her father.

“Do you see that thing up there?” He pointed at the bump. “I want you to take this knife and stab it.”

“Stab it?” she asked. “What for? What is exactly that thing?”

“Just believe me,” he said. “You’ll love this.”

The daughter tried to climb the tree but it was sticky and she slipped many times, then she rather climbed on his father’s back. Once up there, she had the height enough to do as his father asked. She stabbed the thing but it didn’t go through. The outside of it was hard as the older trees. She tried again until the tip started to <span class="fx-sharp">pierce.</span> With a few more tries the whole blade went in.

A golden fluid came from it. It <span data-action="fall">poured away and spilled over them</span>; they were covered by it.

“What is this?" the daughter asked. “Why would you want to be coated in this sticky stuff.”

“Open your mouth,” the father answered with a big laugh. “You have to try it first. This is honey—”

Elana put some of it on her mouth and the sheer amount of new flavor could not be contained in one expression. She went from smiling to almost crying in the blink of a second. <span class="fx-key">The sweet flavor hugged her taste buds and she wanted to keep feeling it forever.</span> She swallowed and put more in her mouth. This was the first time she liked the taste of food.

“Do you feel it?” he said. “I haven’t tried something like this since before I met your mother.”

“This must be what the gods eat,” Elana said. A flavor like that wasn’t even imaginable for her a minute ago and now she couldn't imagine living without it.

“This was said to be one of the Sun’s gifts to the kingdom, this is the kind of wonders why we came here. There will be many things like this in our way to the City of Gods. You can have as you want, this forest is full of it. We’ll pass the night in here.”

They looked around. The once creepy forest was now a nest of delicacies. All those colors of the burned trees and the dim lights they reflected, the twisted roots and the spiky thorns; all of it looked great. If it wasn’t her first time, she would’ve said she’d have enough but she just kept eating. They fed themselves until they could no more and then just a little bit more.

<Silence height="sm" ornament="dot" />

<MoodShift theme="void" label="SOMETHING SMALL IN THE THORNS" />

Once they finished, they got back and set the camp. They were both still covered by honey but they didn’t mind it. They changed their clothes and tried to clean themselves but the sticky sensation stayed with them. The night was nigh and the time to sleep had come. They hadn’t drunk nor eaten yet the dirty water and old grains had never looked so awful. They just ate some more honey and lied next to each other.

Once Elana thought that her father was asleep, she went back in the forest. She walked slowly with her arms in front of her—darkness all around her. Cold breeze, visible breath, snow piling from the storm. She walked around and sat on a fallen tree. She sighed, then smiled.

*The stories are true*, she thought. *The empire does have the wonders my father had told me about. What will await us now?*

Some weeds shook a few meters away. Elana jumped. She was certain that she had not imagined that. *Impossible*, she thought. She didn’t bring a light so she was practically blind. It didn’t matter, she knew that something was there.

More bushes shook nearby. The thing was close. It let out a disturbing sound, one she had never heard. It came from something small, nonetheless she was terrified.

Her knees were <span class="fx-impact">trembling</span>.

She heard it again, this time clearer. Elana did not find a threat in the sound, instead she thought of it as a call for help.

With all the bravery she could gather, she walked towards it. Beneath some bushes, within weeds and thorns. A black creature was screaming in pain. It was small, almost hidden in the dark. Only his bright eyes shined with the light in the distance.

*An animal*, she thought. She recognized the sound of what her father had taught her. It was a cat.

She moved closer, keeping her guard up. She quickly noticed that he was stuck with some branches. With fear, she took the paw and released it. The cat jumped away and got lost in the penumbra, sniffing one of her boots. She extended her arm with caution. The animal was curious, it quickly changed its attention to the moving arm. He sniffed it again and rubbed its face on one finger. Elana shivered and moved her hand away. It stepped back, it was scared too. The touch was soft and warm, not so bad—not bad at all. She approached again.

Something <span data-action="surge">moved fast towards them</span>. It broke branches and trees. It did not care about the thorns and weeds, it certainly didn’t even consider the trunks stopping its way. In a blink, it was gone. The other animal had gone too.

*Did it eat it?* she thought. She wasn’t certain. She just found some animal in the forest and it had disappeared just as fast. Maybe it was her mother?

She stood up and shook the snow off her coat. She felt some fear but felt safe at the same time. Whatever it was, it did not want to harm her.

<span class="fx-whisper">Not yet.</span>

<MoodShift theme="dusk" label="THE NEXT MORNING" />

The next morning Elana woke up first. She went back to the tree and climbed it to get some fresh honey. Elana took a spoon of the sweet liquid and something was different. The taste remained the same but the joy of eating it was no more. She took another spoonful of honey. The novelty had quickly palled and her mouth repudiated the feeling.

She was confused as to why she could not eat more of it. She looked around and met her father's eyes perplexed. They had fed up with the flavor. They knew it without saying a word.

“Time to go?” Elana asked.

“It would seem so— We’ll fill the cart with as much of it as we can carry. Then we go.”

“If the paths are in our favor, we’ll surely reach the nearest town before the tiredness reaches us.” She looked at the trees around them. “It was good while it lasted.”

They took their things and gathered them on the cart, putting all their recipients full of the golden liquid. Elana had never sung as brightly as that day, she was sad for they had to abandon the place, but she never felt an expectation as big for what would come next. When they walked through the roots and thorns, with the faint light they received, the golden flasks shined in a color of hope. They thought that days would get better.

The father coughed.

They walked for hours in the thick forest, the colors from them shined less and less the more they advanced. The trees that stuck together and closed their way were widening until they could see the end. It was a good day, considering all factors. The day wasn’t as cold and the mud wasn’t as wet. They moved easy through the dirt, faster than usual. After six hours of travel, they finally saw the town.

The old man broke in a cough, hard and dry.

“Are you alright, dad?” Elana asked.

“I’m perfectly fine.”

Elana frowned. “Are you sure? We can rest here if you need to.”

“My dear daughter. There’s no need. I’m feeling fantastic, besides, we’re so close to the town. It would be a waste to stop here.” His voice was cracking.

“You can barely talk. We need to do something about it.”

“Come on, it’s just the ashes. I’m fine, believe me.”

Elana said nothing. They entered the town.

A small town in the middle of the Empire, nothing worth noting. They had been in hundreds—if not even thousands—of them. She was bored to look at the buildings, all the same, dull. She once used to draw the houses in her sketchbook, but she realized that it was just a loss of paper, they didn’t deserve the attention.

They walked the empty roads and crumbling buildings. She saw a few bodies on the ground near the open fields that surrounded the small town. They were wearing different armor and garments. Their colors were different and so were their flags. This was a scene of war, fought by the last warriors of the kingdom. Blood stained the entryway.

<MoodShift theme="ash" label="THE COLLAPSE" />

A cough again. Elana looked at her father and he just smiled.

“I’m fine,” he said. As he stopped pushing the wheelbarrow and <span class="fx-impact">fell down</span>. On the floor, on his knees, he held his chest with great discomfort. A cough. An irrepressible cough came from the bottom of his being. He had been trying to hold it in until he could release it away from his daughter. It came with hatred and teared his throat from the inside. A puddle of blood in the floor. Elana saw everything horrified, with a hand covering her mouth, she could not process what was going on. The old man tried to recover his stance, but this was nothing like the normal cough. He tried to stand up using the cart to help himself, but his legs were of no use and his arms had lost their strength. <span class="fx-key">He lay on the floor, next to his blood, staining him in red.</span>

*What?*

“Father!” shouted the girl as she ran to his aid. “What has gotten into you?”

“Oh, Elana, my dear Elana. It seems that time is taking its toll on my insides. Soon I will be no more.”

“What kind of nonsense are you saying?” She held his head with both hands. “You’re as strong as ever and have been traveling as usual for hours. It’s just the fatigue of your bones. You’ll be up in no time after a long night rest.”

“I would love if that was the solution, but I’m afraid it’s not. This was a long time coming. I've known for a while, I felt it years ago and the sensation only grew with time.”

“Why would you not tell me then?” Elana exclaimed. “I would have carried the cart, I would have gotten our food, we would have stayed in the same place for you to rest.”

“There is no use in lamenting. I did what I thought was better for you. This old man doesn’t matter anymore—” He held his daughter’s face and stroked her hair. “Look where we are, this is Oldraan. We did it. This will be your home. The gods are in our favor.”

“I don’t care about this place; <span class="fx-key">I don’t care about the dead gods. All I care about is you!</span>”

She took his father and dragged him inside one of the nearest buildings; it was a residential house. On the first story she found a bed big enough to fit him. She put him there—carefully—and brought some honey and water. On the second floor she found some dirty sheets that she dusted off before offering them to her father. She did the same with a pillow for his head.

*Food*, she thought.

She ran and brought a jar with honey. “Eat this father. It will give you strength.”

He was moving abruptly. She put the jar close to him, but he tossed it away. His cough was worse than ever—blood spilling everywhere.

“I’m sorry child,” he said. “I’m sorry, but I can’t eat.”

<span class="fx-impact">The glass hit the floor and shattered all around.</span> The golden liquid spilled.

He writhed in pain, even when he did his best to control it. He coughed. Elana put some of the fur she wore to keep him warm. He coughed. He could not contain it anymore; his chest was burning. The father held his locket with a firm grip, he wanted to scream but the thought of his little girl suffering for him was greater than any pain. He coughed some more.

Blood, grunts and suffering. The night was long and devoid of sleep. In a corner of the room was sitting Elana, with hope abandoning her eyes.

Why do the gods allow something like this to happen? He was a good man and did not deserve this. She cursed in silence.

*Days will not get better*, she thought. *They never will.* She looked at the shattered jar with the spilling honey. Her whole face was red and tears did not stop falling.

<span class="fx-key">Honey will never taste as sweet as the first time.</span>

<span class="fx-whisper">The taste of the air was bitter.</span>
