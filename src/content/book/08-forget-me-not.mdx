They had kept their pace for a long time. They walked in silence which was awkward for Elana who was used to talk while traveling with her father. She didn’t know how to break the ice. Death, on the other hand, didn't seem to notice and only followed the girl.

After a while, the bleeding Sun started to go down. Only a faint light covered them and they had to stop.

“I’ll make a fire; the night is coming.”

In the arid desert was very few things to use for fire; Elana always carried sticks with her for cases like that. She used them to make a fire big enough to keep her warm. She had another sip of her flask. The water bottle was half-way empty.

“This will keep us warm,” said Elana.

<DeathVoice>
  “You don’t need to worry about my wellbeing. I’m unable to feel the dark and coldness. I do not need to drink or eat. I do not sleep or get tired. That’s for the living.”
</DeathVoice>

“Very well then. We’ll spend the night here and continue tomorrow. In a couple of days, we’ll be out of here.”

Elana laid on the floor and warmed her hands. The night began to get colder. Ruppert was very curious about the girl, he liked her touch as she was always warm. He found his way to her legs where he fell asleep. She petted him gently.

“What's the name of the little one?” asked the girl.

<DeathVoice>
  “He's a cat, it would be fitting to call him that.”
</DeathVoice>

“Are you saying that you didn't give him a proper name?”

<DeathVoice>
  “There's no use in that. He’s a cat; the only cat. No place left for confusion.”
</DeathVoice>

“He needs a name, everyone does.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Then you can choose one as you please.”
</DeathVoice>

Death thought that it was funny how humans were concerned about the little things; no other species did that. It was entertaining for her looking at Elana taking her time to think for a name. She played with the cat and after a few minutes, she knew exactly the name for him.

“Ruppert, that will be his name. Do you like it?”

<DeathVoice>
  “It has a nice sound to it.”
</DeathVoice>

“Then it is decided then.” Her smile got interrupted as she paused for a second. “Wait... Is Death your name?”

<DeathVoice>
  “Not exactly a name but everyone calls me Death.”
</DeathVoice>

“I'm sure someone gave you a name other than death. Is that really how you want to be known?”

<DeathVoice>
  “I've been called many things by many people. I just don’t see the use of it. Why have a name when there will come a day when there's <span class="fx-ghost">no one left to pronounce it?</span>”
</DeathVoice>

“But I’m here to pronounce it now. The fact that something is not everlasting is not enough to not be worthwhile. <span class="fx-key">The joy of life comes from those small things that you know will not last.</span>”

<DeathVoice>
  “I'm not alive, therefore that doesn't apply to my existence.”
</DeathVoice>

“You well know what I mean.”

They were both sitting together at the trunk when Elana hit Death with her elbow. Even Ruppert stopped playing and looked at them.

<DeathVoice>
  “Then I want to be called like the blue flowers.”
</DeathVoice>

“Flowers? My father had told me about them but not me nor him had seen one.”

<DeathVoice>
  “It was long before your time when <span class="fx-stretch">hills and mountains stood high.</span> The meadows were filled with colors but the most beautiful to my eyes was always blue.”
</DeathVoice>

Death opened her bag and from it she took out a dry blue flower. It was the same flower she had taken away a few weeks prior. Death carefully took care of it to not break any petal and showed it to Elana.

<DeathVoice>
  “It brings me good memories.”
</DeathVoice>

“It's truly beautiful. I would have loved to see it alive. I see why you like them.”

<DeathVoice>
  “It's not only that, there were many things beautiful in the past, but nothing like this. This reminds me of someone I met, the smells of the garden and the breeze of the mountains.”
</DeathVoice>

“And what is their name?”

<DeathVoice>
  “They were called forget me nots. <span class="fx-key">They were given as a promise of remembrance.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

“Remembrance? While I like the story, I don't know if it will suit you. It has to be something only you have so people can remember it easily.”

<MoodShift theme="bone" label="A YOUNGER WORLD" />

Death understood what she was saying and thought and remembered a bit more. She saw the <span class="fx-stretch">blue skies</span> in front of her, on her feet she felt the soft and warm dirt. This was a younger world, with a big bright Sun shining on it.

A woman was jumping while singing a cute melody and Death did nothing but hear it. The lady was as cheerful as her age allowed and her beauty only matched her captivating voice. With a long white dress and a braided blond hair, the woman was picking up flowers at Sunset.

Her singing got an orchestra that accompanied her mood. <span class="fx-chime">Strings and choirs resonated with her.</span> Death's bell urged her to action but she wanted to hear it for a bit longer. It didn't last long because when the woman had filled her basket and turned to come back home, she saw Death sitting in front of her.

The basket fell to the floor and the woman tried to pick it up, she was shaking.

“I didn't know you'd come today, otherwise I would have done something to offer you.”

Death remained silent.

“How much time do I have left? I was cooking a pie for my son and I don't want it to burn. If you would allow it, I'd like to wait until it's done. It will be hard for him to mourn my death but maybe eating it will bring him some peace.”

Death was surprised. Most people tried to escape her, others to fight her and very few accepted their fate. It was certainly uncommon for a dying soul to think of others first, specially while they heard the sound of their life going away.

She allowed it and made it clear by showing her the way to her home. Once in the kitchen, the woman left the basket on the table and waited for the pie to be ready. They both sat facing each other.

“Would you like something to eat?” asked the woman. “I'm sure that your job becomes tiring quite fast.”

She didn't even wait for an answer and was already pouring some tea.

“You'll have to excuse me but you'll have to wait if you want some of the pie.” The woman got up and took a letter that was addressed to his son. “I knew the day was coming, my mother died at the same age. I already had everything prepared beforehand.”

Death stared at her; she was fascinated. The old lady took some of the blue flowers and put them inside the envelope.

“You look like you want to ask something... Is it about the flowers? They are forget-me-nots and are given to the people you most care about in the world. It works like a promise, I'll remember him wherever I go and I hope he remembers me back. <span class="fx-key">Do you have someone you want to remember?</span>”

<MoodShift theme="dusk" label="A PROMISE OF REMEMBRANCE" />

<DeathVoice>
  “Odeia. <span class="fx-key">That's how I want to be remembered. That's my name.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

“I really like it,” said Elana. “Then, from now on I'll call you Odeia.”

She tried to give back the flower she'd been holding the whole time, but Odeia refused with her head.

<DeathVoice>
  “I want you to have it.”
</DeathVoice>

said Odeia, and with it she made a promise. <span class="fx-key">She would not forget, no matter the time that goes away, everything she had seen and heard.</span> She will carry on the memories of the world and she would sing the melodies that came from it.

She <span class="fx-chime">started to hum</span> and <span class="fx-whisper">the sound of it tasted like pie.</span>
