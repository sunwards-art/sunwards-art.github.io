It was a long and sleepless night. The room was in absolute silence, only interrupted by the incessant coughing. Amid sheets of blood lay the sick man, spitting his guts out while he slept. Elana by his right, extremely tired after hours of worry, had fallen asleep too. She was holding his father's hands and would not let go. They finally rested.

The old man woke himself up with his own coughs. A dry and gritty cough, one after another. He tried to silence the noise to not wake his daughter up, who was peacefully resting by his side. He turned to the white ceiling seeing the cracks, the paint falling off, the mold and dripping leaks. He sighed. Glanced at all the corners in the room, like trying to count them. He examined the room thoroughly and came up with a plan.

He turned back to Elana—who was just as beautiful as the day she was born—wanting her image to dwell in his mind. He recognized her mother in her, as if they shared a soul. A smile, a glance, a tender touch. They were so similar that he believed she had been living with them for all these years. He had memorized her appearance, embodied in his memory, but he glanced at her even more. After seventeen years, Elana remained the sole thing in his eyes. He didn’t want to stop admiring her for a second, but the dreadful thought of what would come troubled his mind.

He took out his locket and opened it. Inside, a drawing of his love beside a broken clock. A beautiful woman of red hair, the same Elana had. Even when the portrait was not as detailed, one could confuse their faces. He closed it and placed it back, next to his heart. He was prepared now, or so he thought. With slow movements, he woke his daughter up.

“Elana, my darling,” he mumbled with struggle. “I have to ask for a favor of you.”

“Absolutely, father. You can ask for anything.”

“I’m thirsty, my mouth is dry. Could you get me some water?”

“I'll go now. But you finished all we had left. I’ll have to boil some first and that will take a bit.”

“Worry not, my dear. I won’t go anywhere.”

He knew very well how much water they had left; he also knew how much it would take her to bring some more. He counted the minutes that she would be away. When Elana stood up and approached the door, he delayed her.

“Elana,” he said with his voice failing. “I’m very proud of how much you’ve grown and I’m sure your mother would be as well. I know how difficult things seem now, but time will make it easier. I’ve always admired how you keep pushing forward, and that’s what you… what we should do. Always advance, one day at a time. <span class="fx-key">This hope you have is what gives you strength, don’t forget it.</span>”

“I won’t, dad. I learned it all from you,” she answered just about to leave his sight. “We have come so close; we’ll finally see what your stories talk about. The city of gods will welcome us very soon. Now, stay there and stop talking. I’ll be back.”

“Wait, I have to tell you something,” he mumbled out of breath. His mouth was dry, no words came out. <span class="fx-whisper">“I love you, darling. I’m sorry,”</span> he whispered.

Elana did not hear him; she had gone too far away. He was exhausted, and his voice gave up with the final words. He stayed cursing in silence for a few minutes.

With all the strength he had left, he stood up and dragged himself to the entrance. He locked the door and used a chair to block it. His body could move no more and dropped to the cold floor. His coughing returned and worsened. His chest hurt and burned; his insides were already destroyed. He lay on the floor, hopeless, waiting for his fate.

<MoodShift theme="void" label="THE BELL RINGS IN THE ROOM" />

<span class="fx-chime">A bell rang and echoed in the room.</span> On the opposite side of where he stood, a mass of dust was forming. From the corner itself, dust came together to become a tall and slender figure. It had four arms and had to bend its body to fit in there. A soft breeze came with it, bringing sounds and smells of other places. He felt the chills on his skin, and <span class="fx-chime">the song of a bell filled his ears.</span> The father was not surprised with this entrance, in fact, he just watched it with interest.

When the dust had all come into one piece, Death showed her entire figure. She stepped towards the old man holding the small cat with her lower pair of arms. At the first sight of him, she honored his presence with a nod of her head, and he nodded back.

*I’ve known this moment would come for twenty-three years*, the man thought. *Yet my eyes still can’t tell if they are being deceived. I have dreamed of my death since I first felt the illness seven years ago.*

On the floor, the man had a good look of that imposing figure, the same he remembered of many years ago. In front of him, she bent to get to his height and stared directly at him. The thin fingers wrapped him, and grabbed him. He <span class="fx-impact">shook at the cold touch</span> but could not resist the grip, he just let his body loose. With care he was put back on bed. She got a change of sheets and a soft pillow.

The day finally came.

“Greetings, old Death,” the old man said still shaking, “I know you mean no harm. I’ve been waiting for your visit. Though I have to say that I expected you would come years ago.”

“I see.” He relaxed his words. “Then I guess that it has come. By any chance, do you remember me?”

<DeathVoice>
  “I’ve performed my duty with many souls. It’s hard to remember specifics. In the past, my mind wasn’t focused, and I didn’t use to pay attention. Have we met before, lone traveler? For the look of your eyes, it seems like we have.”
</DeathVoice>

“I saw you take my uncle away; he was a good warrior and great musician. Then you came again the day my daughter was born. You took her mother away. Part of me died with her, and I’ve been waiting for you ever since. If it weren't for Elana, I would have given up long ago.”

Death frowned.

<DeathVoice>
  “My apologies for my forgetfulness. I do not remember those who you speak of, I know they were as brave as you. You have noted what I ought to complete already. Worry not, you won’t experience any pain. No more than what you have endured already. Just close your eyes.”
</DeathVoice>

“I’m sorry to bother you, but I need to know. What will happen to the world?”

*You are a bold man*, she thought.

Not many people asked her questions, of course she answered none. But this human was different, he didn’t have greed on his eyes, his look was rather of concern. She didn’t feel hesitant to reply. She knew he had good intent.

<DeathVoice>
  “You don’t have to worry about that anymore. There will be no more suffering for you.”
</DeathVoice>

“But I do. I have a daughter, and I have to know what will become of her. So, I implore you, wise being, tell me. What will be of this world after I’m gone?”

<DeathVoice>
  “It’s dying and fading. It doesn’t have much time, it seems. Without the gods, things aren’t going to change. Days will get colder and shorter. Dark will come. In the near future all life will be gone, but what’s to come is unknown—even for me.”
</DeathVoice>

“Why, powerful being, first of the death. Is there something you can do to change this?”

<DeathVoice>
  “Nothing can be done. It is not in my power, at least. We are approaching the end of all things. The natural conclusion to our existence.”
</DeathVoice>

“Will my daughter have more time?”

She looked at him. His afraid stare made her uncomfortable. She sighed with sadness.

<DeathVoice>
  “Most probably she will. It’s come to my attention that the last few souls have been the more resilient. You have lasted more than you should have. Humans seem to be stronger of what they look like. You must not worry. Like you, she will enjoy a long life. Not a single being will see the world collapse at the end, I assure you of that.”
</DeathVoice>

“You say ‘enjoy’, as if that's even possible on times of penury. I know it won’t be easy for her but I just don’t want her to suffer.”

<DeathVoice>
  “It is hard to separate life from suffering.”
</DeathVoice>

He stopped for a second. He already felt more comfortable with the talk, or so Death saw in his eyes. The man was not covering himself anymore. “Can I request a favor of you?”

The goddess nodded.

<DeathVoice>
  “You may tell me the favor, old man.”
</DeathVoice>

“Elana, my daughter is a good girl. She’s brave but she doesn't have enough experience on her own. She has been the only reason for me to continue and my soul would crumble if harm comes to her. I can’t die without knowing that she’ll be fine. <span class="fx-key">Could you take care of her?</span> I know that the gods are gone but she's the only evidence that they ever existed, she needs to be protected.”

<DeathVoice>
  “I can’t promise such a thing. I can’t take care of a life.”
</DeathVoice>

“Yet you already do.” The old man was pointing at the cat on her arms.

*Not only blunt, but also clever*, she thought.

<DeathVoice>
  “I don’t take care of him; we merely share our ways.”
</DeathVoice>

“Then share your ways with her, my lady. When I die, I’m certain she’ll try to reach the city of gods, but the path is full of horrors. She won’t make it on her own.”

<DeathVoice>
  “It is not possible. I wouldn’t—”
</DeathVoice>

“Just until she reaches the city,” he interrupted her. “I implore you. It is my sole wish for her to live. Nothing else matters anymore. This is the last wish of this dying man. I’m sure that your immense power can take her there.”

The goddess took her time to think. He expected her answer with eagerness. <span class="fx-chime">The bells started to rush in his ears.</span> She got near him and made the string produce sound. From the thread came multiple voices that joined the intricate song; it was a chorus. He could feel it, a stream of feelings and memories coming from it. It was the sound of his life.

The chorus was accompanied by multiple noises coming from the bell. Many sounds came together in that beautiful cacophony. Pleasant voices sang along when Death played the silver string again. <span class="fx-key">This was a song of hope and care.</span> He heard the tears of a mother, the proud pat on the shoulder of an uncle, the last kiss of a lover, and the hope of a daughter. All the people he knew were in there, resounding on his ears, vibrating on his soul.

*They were always with me*, he thought.

</DeathVoice>

“Dear and just Death, my heart will be eternally in gratitude with your kindness. You’re genuinely a wish granter, but there is one more thing I have to ask of you.”

  “One more thing?”
</DeathVoice>

she asked and gripped her scissors strongly.

“She witnessed when you took her mother away, so she’ll be able to see you. If you come to her now, she’ll know that you took me and hate you for it.”

<DeathVoice>

</DeathVoice>

“I don’t think that’s a good idea. If anything, it would only make it worse.”

<DeathVoice>

The goddess sighed.

<DeathVoice>

“Why, old Death, you’re more than kind. I can’t thank you enough.”
