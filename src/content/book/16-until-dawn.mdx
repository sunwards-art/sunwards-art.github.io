Ruppert was left on the ground. Elana tried to help him, but there was little to do. He was barely breathing.

Odeia got closer and had a look.

<DeathVoice>
  “It seems like the ribs are fractured. They probably pierced an organ.”
</DeathVoice>

“Is there anything you can do?”

<DeathVoice>
  “No.”
</DeathVoice>

Odeia sighed.

<DeathVoice>
  “Not to my knowledge. I can’t cure this. He will perish.”
</DeathVoice>

“Just like that? After everything we went through, you’ll let him die like this? This is not his time. Not yet.”

Odeia put a hand to her shoulder.

<DeathVoice>
  “You know—more than anyone—that it will happen eventually.”
</DeathVoice>

“I know—” Elana said in tears. Her voice was trembling. “I know, but it doesn’t make it less painful.”

<DeathVoice>
  “No, it does not. This is truly heartbreaking; he was still full of life.”
</DeathVoice>

That sentence made things worse. She knew that he was meant to live for much longer, but he preferred saving them. He was the true hero.

Ruppert closed his eyes—as if letting them know that he accepted his fate—he was stopping his fight. His body got tense on Elana’s hand as she felt his life go away.

*I’m sorry*, she thought. *I could not help you. I didn’t protect you.*

“Do it before it’s too late,” Elana said in a rush.

Odeia nodded. Speed was necessary. She could not tell him anything, she could not calm him. He was too weak at the moment, probably unconscious already. Odeia <span class="fx-slash">cut the faint thread</span> and freed him from any suffering. His essence <span data-action="rise">ascended and went to the skies</span>. <span class="fx-key">The last cat was gone and a small part of Elana died with him.</span>

<DeathVoice>
  “This is not fair. I’m sorry that you had to see him go. We couldn’t even give him our farewell.”
</DeathVoice>

“Life is never fair,” Elana noted. “It’s full of ups and downs, life and death. We—the last ones on the world—can only do but keep the memories of who preceded us alive.”

The goddess just nodded.

“Did you hear it?” Elana asked.

<DeathVoice>
  “Hear what, exactly?”
</DeathVoice>

“<span class="fx-chime">The song that came from him.</span> It was beautiful.”

Odeia looked surprised.

<DeathVoice>
  “It was. He had a pure essence. The world will sound emptier without him.”
</DeathVoice>

“That is what you hear every time you cut a thread?”

<DeathVoice>
  “Yes. A melody only audible on the last minutes of life.”
</DeathVoice>

“It makes the end bittersweet. That was the most beautiful sound I’ve ever heard. It’s sad to know that you’ll die, but dying while hearing that sound can’t be so bad. Like a sweet aftertaste at the end.”

<DeathVoice>
  “A reward worthy for few.”
</DeathVoice>

Elana took the small body on her arms. *He looks so fragile*, she thought, *yet he saved us. Rest in peace, friend.* She stood up. “We need to bury him.”

<MoodShift theme="dusk" label="ATOP THE CAPITOL" />

Both went to the top of the Capitol. The stairs seemed endless, with rooms and halls to spare. Elana carried Ruppert all the way. Once they got out, the Sun shined over them—still bleeding.

“I want to bury him here,” Elana said. “What’s left of the Sun will touch him when it dawns and darkens. I think he would’ve liked that. He liked warmth.” She was smiling, covered in tears but smiling. She didn’t see death as something bad anymore.

“I understand why my dad used to smile so often,” the girl said.

<DeathVoice>
  “He was a jovial man.”
</DeathVoice>

“Not exactly. I see it now, he was afraid. Afraid of so many things he didn’t have control over. And the only thing he could do was smile. He was not lying when he did it. He was trying to convince himself. Like a self-promise to give his best. I still feel warm when I think about it. That’s why I’ll smile, even when I’m sad. Especially when I’m sad. I can’t kill the pain, but pain doesn’t kill our moments together either. Focus on the bad feelings would be a betrayal. <span class="fx-key">His memory is now stronger than his absence.</span> His teachings will live through me.”

*I carry him inside me.*

Odeia remained silent. Elana wiped her face. She took her sword and made a hole in the ground, putting Ruppert inside it. Then she raked the earth to cover the hole. When she finished, she <span class="fx-slash">sliced her hair</span> with her sword.

<DeathVoice>
  “Why do you cut your hair?”
</DeathVoice>

“Some part of me will always be with him.” She stood up and shook off the dirt. Her sword—that was mostly useless then—was nailed in the soil in front of the burial. She took the locket off her neck and hanged it on the guard of the sword. Open, the locket showed the portraits of both of her parents.

“This is the place my father wanted to be. He dedicated most of his life to find it. We traveled for seven years. And now I fulfilled his wish. He reached the city of gods.”

<DeathVoice>
  “His real wish was for you to be happy.”
</DeathVoice>

“I know.”

<DeathVoice>
  “You like the locket. Why leave it here? Take it with you.”
</DeathVoice>

“They’ll like the company and the sunlight during mornings.” Elana put a hand to her heart. “I don’t need a physical object to remember them. I do it daily already. They are here.”

Elana looked back at her and smiled. “Do you want to say anything?”

The goddess was resting her body on her sword, behind the girl.

<DeathVoice>
  “He’s gone. He will not hear us now.”
</DeathVoice>

“I know, but the words aren’t only for him. It’s our goodbye. To close our cycle together, to remember him.”

<DeathVoice>
  “You humans have weird rituals,”
</DeathVoice>

Odeia noted. She knelt and touched the soil.

<DeathVoice>
  <span class="fx-ritual">“I would have loved to be there for him when he needed me the most, to reassure him. I failed him. But there are a few things that I would like him to know. He showed me that life was worth preserving.</span> <span class="fx-key">He showed me that beauty thrives even in the most precarious conditions.</span> <span class="fx-ritual">I am truly thankful for that. He was the beginning step to understand my path.”</span>
</DeathVoice>

She went back to her position. Still looking down, she cried in silence.

<DeathVoice>
  “He was soft, not only in body, but in soul. He brought us together, bonded with us. Without him, we would not be here. I’ll miss him every morning. <span class="fx-whisper">Good night.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

“Good night,” Elana repeated.

And the night passed quieter than the previous day.

<MoodShift theme="bone" label="THE QUIET YEARS" />

Life was easier after the fight with the emperor—boring even. There were no risks or danger. Elana was safe.

They found huge amounts of supplies in the throne room, the emperor had enough food to last for millennia, maybe more. The palace was warm, so she didn’t have any concern about weather anymore. All the worries she and her father ever had were gone. She had a quiet life.

Odeia taught the girl how to read and write. She spent her days learning more about the people before her, it was her passion. She also wrote all the events of the Unlit Days, things Odeia told her, things she had witnessed. She enjoyed the stories as much as she had enjoyed the pictures. The walls were covered in paintings, works which Elana carefully studied. In a few years she could replicate them in perfection. Then she learned to do her own. She tried to teach the goddess how to do it, only to find that she had a perfect skill, but Odeia never found the value of art. *It is the expression of the soul*, the girl used to say. Her thoughts and memories colored the palace and streets of the city. She felt that the gold was too monotonous for her taste after some years. Elana always found something new to do.

The goddess enjoyed her time with the girl. A few years ago, she had longed for the bell to ring, now she knew how to spend her time. Boredom was a thing of the past. Elana’s company was very much to her liking, but she also liked to be alone, lost in her thoughts. She became—for the surprise of Elana—more conversational with time. Some days they talked until dawn, she used to go for hours with stories about people she liked.

Elana grew into a young woman, one who was still curious about the world. Odeia told her about the creation of times and the beginning of life. The goddess—despite her status—knew very little about those things. Elana had to base her knowledge on old religious texts, words that often contradicted each other. She didn’t mind though, she found wisdom in said words. She loved all types of knowledge, even the useless one. She filled her head with facts that would help her with very little. She knew about the gods, the life of giants, the kingdoms of men, the cycles of crops, the colors of flowers.

All knowledge holds some value.

She passed her years reading and talking with Odeia. Often beside a window, lit by the dim light of the red Sun. She learned to love the sunsets with the color she once hated. Never noticing it before, the death of night brought new colors to the shadows. She loved to see the old Sun go down and up each day, staying late to see it, accompanied by some melody of Odeia.

Elana grew old. Her bright red hair was white as the snow. Her body wasn’t as strong anymore and she needed help to move around. Odeia happily helped, it wasn’t an inconvenience for her.

One day, the old woman asked to get to a special place.

<DeathVoice>
  “The top of the palace?”
</DeathVoice>

“Yes. I’d like to see the sunset there. It will be astonishing tonight.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Of course, I can take you there.”
</DeathVoice>

*What would I do without you?* Elana asked herself.

<DeathVoice>
  “You are perfectly capable for yourself.”
</DeathVoice>

“Wha—How did you know—”

<DeathVoice>
  “I know you. Words aren’t always necessary.”
</DeathVoice>

“Do you read minds now?”

<DeathVoice>
  “No, but I can read you,”
</DeathVoice>

she said smiling.

On the goddess’s back, held by her lower set of arms, traveled Elana. As they ascended, the Sun began to hide behind the hills. The gold spikes on the top shined brightly. The skies were clear and Atorus looked at them from above.

Elana sighed at the view. “Isn’t this amazing?”

<DeathVoice>
  “I can’t believe you have seen the same sunset for years and still like it with this much fervor.”
</DeathVoice>

“The little things,” Elana added. “I’ve learned to appreciate them.”

<DeathVoice>
  “The little things,”
</DeathVoice>

Odeia repeated while looking at the horizon.

“I’ve heard the bells all day, you know? Their sound is quite calming. I don’t know how much you’ll wait but I’m ready.”

<DeathVoice>
  “I know. I just wanted you to see this. A last time. I used to give long speeches when I used to do this, but words aren’t enough for you.”
</DeathVoice>

“A warm feeling and a hug are all I need. You have said enough throughout the years.”

They hugged. A long hug that wanted to last forever. They rested their foreheads together and stayed in that position for minutes without a word.

“I almost forgot it,” Elana said. “I brought this for you.”

It was the flower that Odeia gave her all those years ago. It was withered but well kept. Still holding some colors.

“I want you to have it.”

<DeathVoice>
  “It is yours.”
</DeathVoice>

“You said once that this works as a promise. <span class="fx-key">I will remember you and I hope that you remember me back, Odeia.</span> Our time together was quite lovely.”

The goddess took the flower as the Sun went down; her eyes were tearing up. Elana’s smile was barely visible with the low light. The time had come.

“What will you do without me?” Elana asked smiling as she used a finger to wipe the tears.

<DeathVoice>
  “Little things,”
</DeathVoice>

Odeia said laughing.

She grabbed the golden scissors and made the thread sound.

Odeia was clouded by the sensation. Her whole body <span class="fx-chime">vibrated along the song.</span> She knew most of it already—a song of a sweet child in a harsh world. The mother whispered, the father sang, the world gave the chorus and Odeia hummed along. The sound resumed all their talks; all their time together; the books she had read; the people she had known; the cold and hunger she felt. In her weak thread was the last soul in the world. Elana felt all those things coming from her. Her eyes were raining, but she never stopped smiling.

<DeathVoice>
  “Goodnight.”
</DeathVoice>

<span class="fx-key">“Until dawn,”</span> replied Elana.

<MoodShift theme="void" label="ALONE" />

The thread was cut, and <span class="fx-ghost">the sound died with it.</span> The silence of the world was crushing her. Odeia remained speechless.

She sobbed uncontrollably that night.

<span class="fx-whisper">She was alone.</span>
