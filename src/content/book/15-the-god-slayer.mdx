A deep voice came from the throne. “Oh, the Sun’s pawn is here. I didn’t imagine that you would take this long.”

*The Sun’s pawn?* Odeia thought.

<DeathVoice>
  “My name is Odeia, you will refer to me as such.”
</DeathVoice>

“Excuse my manners, my lady. As you see, a millennia of solitude can break a man's mind.” The voice of the emperor was dry but loud. It was hard to imagine that his frail body could produce a sound like that.

Odeia stopped for a second.

<DeathVoice>
  “There are many things under the skin and dust, but a man is not one of them. What are you doing with the Sun’s blood? Explain yourself.”
</DeathVoice>

“My lady,” said the decrepit man. “Whatever the Sun commanded, I accomplished, the same that you did.” He pointed at the dripping blood. “However, I’m no more than a servant of the people, I only did what was better for the empire. I’m not his pawn anymore, not like you.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Nonsense. I was never his pawn. I never followed orders.”
</DeathVoice>

“But you were his most powerful weapon all this time.” He did not continue once he looked at her head and neck. She wasn’t wearing her father's gifts. He laughed. “It seems like you know already. Don’t worry, it took me decades to escape from his schemes. I don’t blame you.”

<DeathVoice>
  “I don’t know what you are referring to.”
</DeathVoice>

“What else could I be talking about? He used you, my lady. He created you to deal with those pesky humans that rebelled against him.” He laughed again, with his crackly voice and dry mouth. “He always had a temper with rebels.”

<DeathVoice>
  “I did no such thing. I just saw him once, he never ordered me who should die. It was the threads who told me.”
</DeathVoice>

“And you knew that using the bell, didn’t you? You followed its sound.”

Odeia didn’t answer. Elana just looked at them.

“What—or who to be precise—did you think made it ring. He made you kill for him. Anyone who reached too much power, anyone who defied him. You just did as he pleased. I also fell for his lies.”

<DeathVoice>
  “It is your filthy mouth that is full of lies—”
</DeathVoice>

“Don’t be naive. If you do not believe that my words carry at least the smallest amount of truth, then you would have his scissors on your hand. You also know that he used us.”

<DeathVoice>
  “I won’t listen to anything you have to say. After all, you were the one who slayed the Sun, isn’t that a fact?”
</DeathVoice>

“Kill him? Oh, for the Sun’s name, no. I didn't kill him, he was more useful to me alive—even when I had to follow him blindly. But come on, I think we can all agree that he was a jerk to us all. No one will miss him, there’s no one left to do so—” He laughed again, a twisted laugh that only could come from a mad man. “It was his own people that killed him, I only use his blood. Why waste something so useful?”

<DeathVoice>
  “Liar! I can feel the divine essence beneath you, you have killed the gods—maybe all of them—and took their power for you.”
</DeathVoice>

“There’s no longer a need to lie. It is true that I’ve killed some deities, but not the Sun, he was more than I could take. I killed Atorus—by his command—then I slayed the moon.”

“Monster,” Elana told him. “Why would you commit such atrocities? The world will die because of you.”

The emperor ignored her presence. “I was chosen by him to expand his empire and bring him the souls of the other gods. It is a tragedy that he died before I gave him the power. He could have survived otherwise.”

<DeathVoice>
  “You killed all gods.”
</DeathVoice>

She was beginning to feel something burn inside her—the same feeling she had on the Cathedral.

“Yes. I was the protector of his reign and took the best course of action to preserve it. The death of the gods was inevitable.”

Elana was astonished, jaw dropped. Odeia could not believe that only a man could gain this amount of power.

<DeathVoice>
  “Then why didn’t you protect him?”
</DeathVoice>

“After I took the essence, I became wise. Taking divinity upon myself opened my eyes to his wrongdoing—he was a tyrant. He had to go down. He was the only one who could beat me in battle, that’s why I let him die.”

<DeathVoice>
  “You are the greediest of men that I have known.”
</DeathVoice>

“There is no greed on my actions. I did this for the people. I dreamt of a world where men ruled themselves, no gods or kings. I’ll use the power of the essence to recreate this world—only better.”

<DeathVoice>
  “A world where you could rule? I will not let you. I’m still here, my essence is strong.”
</DeathVoice>

“Indeed, I’ve been waiting. That’s why I hid here, a dome with no corners for you to enter. I knew that the Sun’s blood would hide my thread. After all, I saw what happened when people of Deilos consumed Atorus’s blood.”

<DeathVoice>
  “So, it was you. You damned the souls of those men, you killed the world, <span class="fx-key">you are responsible for poisoning their minds and killing their souls. Now, you will die.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

“Again, acting on the Sun’s command. Why can’t you see the truth? He wants you to do this. Instead, let's join our forces. Let this world die and create a new one.”

<DeathVoice tremor={true}>
  “I no longer act on the Sun’s whims. I will kill, not because he wants me to, but because I wish to do so.”
</DeathVoice>

It was the first time that Odeia felt hatred, the first time she wished someone to die. Her sight became obscured and her mind foggy. On the world, there were only the two of them and only one would survive.

“Kill me? I am the new god of this world, I can’t die,” the emperor said loudly as he took a sip of the leaking blood. Suddenly he didn’t look as weak. His thin complexion grew, marking his muscles. His crooked posture fixed and made him look taller. He stood up, showing a body that no man could have, but he was no man. He jumped in the air landing behind Odeia. He was faster than her, she barely saw him. He had a flaming spear gravitating around him, the one the Sun had used. On his hand a sword, another killer of gods. The spear—embedded in fire—flew against her, while he struck with the sword. She had to be precise while moving or she would have been impaled.

The room grew hotter. It was the power coming out of him—Sun’s energy. He threw sunrays at Odeia, but she knew how to avoid them. The rays melted the ground and carbonized everything else. The power that had been used against entire armies was now firing at her; if it touched her, she was dead.

The spear loosed against her, then it came back to the emperor and flew at her again. The movements were fast as he did not have to physically hold it, on the other hand he was slower with the sword. Odeia felt her skin burn, even when she was untouched, but what boiled was her anger.

“What will you do now? You don’t have your Sun to protect you. Without his weapons you are nothing.”

He was right. The emperor had the advantage, armed with the Sun’s spear and a god slayer. Odeia didn’t want to call her scissors, she had disregarded them for a reason. They brought death to people, they were made to please the Sun. Now she knew the truth, he was using her, and calling her weapons would betray what she now believed.

*No*, she thought. *I didn’t kill because I was a pawn; it was a necessity. The world cannot be filled with souls, they can’t dwell in it forever. Everyone must die.* Odeia understood her purpose. Even when the motives for her creation were tainted, she had found a new meaning on her duty. Cleaning the world—specially from souls that deserved it. Souls like the emperor’s.

*They are my weapons. Not the Sun’s. I made them mine, made them serve for a greater purpose. If cutting threads was only because the Sun wanted it, then why did the bell keep sounding when he died.*

<span class="fx-key">*No, the scissors weren’t a weapon. They are a tool.*</span>

She extended her arm and called them. She could feel their power getting closer, but they were far and would take some time to arrive.

“You give up too easily,” the emperor said. “I can’t believe that in less than a minute you already call for help. What kind of god are you? So desperate, so useless.”

He was ruthless with his attacks. Moving with the grace of Anhelia and the strength of the Sun. He used Atorus’s power to slow down the goddess. Odeia was tired. She felt her movements heavier than usual, struggling even to stay up. He didn’t leave her a second to rest or even regain her stance. The sunrays dissolved everything on their way, moving uncontrollably around her. She was barely avoiding the strikes.

Elana tried to take Ruppert out of harm, but the fast-moving fight made it hard to find a safe place. The rays went in all directions and they didn’t only burn what they touched, also pulverizing what was closer by the mere heat.

The souls were hitting the walls outside the dome, they wanted to enter—for the revenge they couldn’t have in life. Odeia knew then that their unfinished business was not with the Sun—or at least, not anymore—but the emperor himself. He was the one who destroyed villages and kingdoms alike. He caused the land to flatten; made the nights dark; he was, in a way, responsible for the unlit days. Everything revolved back to him.

And he didn’t care.

Odeia was lost in her mind, she kept moving around and dodging attacks. She was fast, but somehow the emperor was even faster. None of his strikes fully connected, but some started to cut her superficially. Her rage made her attacks unrefined, grunting and punching without much aim. She used her four arms at once, making her attacks lose force.

She had her complete focus on the fight, giving her all to defeat him, but she had forgotten that alone she was not. On the other side of the room Elana and Ruppert were trying to dodge the rays flying around. They could barely do so.

Odeia felt the power of Atorus on her—stronger than before. Her movement was impaired, she was slow. <span class="fx-impact">A crushing force struck her from above,</span> making her fall to her knees.

“Can you feel it?” the emperor asked. “The power I possess is unmatchable. Not even the Sun could defeat me. Give up now and keep some of your dignity.”

*Power*, she thought, *is the doom of men. It breaks their heart and blurs their soul. Give the most honest man a reign and come back to see a tyrant.*

Even when she had to fight for her life and her friends, she couldn’t help but feel sad. He had lost everything on his search for divinity, he took the world upon himself and Odeia knew how that felt. She shed a single tear—not one of pity, but true sadness. He could only find rest in death.

And death shall come.

But for that she would have to defeat him, something that seemed impossible.

He threw the spear at her, blocking her movement, and punched her in the face. On the floor, she couldn’t do much to dodge. A quick kick to the abdomen threw her to the ground. When she tried to look up, he kicked her again. She had defeat in her eyes.

“You were the last barrier against a new world, without you, an omnipotent god will be born. ME.”

Elana looked horrified how she took the hits. Odeia was powerless against a monstrosity like that. He was the god slayer. By the look of her eyes, the girl didn’t know what to do. Her knees <span class="fx-impact">trembled</span> and couldn’t move. Ruppert—who was being held by her—jumped down and ran towards Odeia. He jumped at the back of the emperor and scratched his way to his head. He was as fast as the night.

A grunt of pain. The cat had attacked the eye and it started to bleed. <span class="fx-key">Gods don’t bleed.</span>

Odeia had needed someone as small as Ruppert to remind her of that. She could still defeat the emperor. The battle was not lost, as long as she had hope.

*I gave the old man my word*, Odeia thought. *I will protect Elana with my life.* Her hatred dissipated; her heart filled with memories of the time they had spent together. She wanted her to be safe and for that she needed to be calm. She took a deep breath, and looked up. She would not give up.

*Not as long as I can fight.*

The emperor <span data-action="surge">took the cat and made him fly away</span>. <span class="fx-impact">Ruppert hit the wall</span> and <span data-action="fall">slid to the floor</span>, leaving a trail of blood. He was breathing with struggle. Elana ran to help him.

The emperor was bleeding badly. He had lost an eye.

“Even with half of my sight, I can see the future,” the emperor said. “I can see you all three underground, laying the foundation for my new empire.”

“Finish him,” Elana said. “Make him pay for what he’s done.”

Odeia was still on the ground, remembering that she was not alone. Her mind cleared.

<DeathVoice>
  “He will pay. <span class="fx-ritual">No one on Atorus will live unaccounted for their acts. Not while I live.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

She extended her arm to the side. The scissors came to her; the bell, bag and crown flew behind. They made their way through the thick layers of metal in the dome, <span class="fx-cleave">leaving a huge opening on the side</span>. The goddess stood up and took position to fight back.

“Nothing changes,” the emperor said. “I have more power; you already lost this fight.”

<DeathVoice>
  “No. You lost this fight a millennia ago. All your actions have led to this moment. You will go down today.”
</DeathVoice>

She was pointing at the opening behind him. The lamenting souls entering the dome and getting around him. He hadn’t noticed them, too occupied self-praising.

The souls gathered in a circle and ran towards the emperor. They used their weapons to fight him, but the twisted man had no trouble vaporizing their efforts.

Odeia raised her golden scissors.

<DeathVoice>
  “<span class="fx-sharp">This is the end.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

Mistcrawlers broke into the room. Growling, screaming, shouting, their noise filled with pain. They ran to attack the emperor who had a look of genuine fear. They were bigger than usual, like they had come together in an effort to defeat the corrupted man.

“This is all you have?” the emperor asked while radiating his energy to dissolve the souls. The spear moved against the mistcrawlers, cutting them in half. They separated and formed again, not stopping their attacks. The emperor had to step back, he cut them and sliced them but they always formed back.

<DeathVoice>
  “You caused this, don’t forget it. They are the faces of the people you say you wanted to save.”
</DeathVoice>

She used the pair of scissors to charge against him. He was cornered, with weapons pointing at him from all directions. He still kept the pace though, showing no sign of fatigue.

“You can’t kill me,” the emperor said.

<DeathVoice>
  “No, I can’t. But I have something that you don’t. Something that you underestimate.”
</DeathVoice>

“And what is that?” he asked laughing as he obliterated the souls in front of him.

<DeathVoice>
  “I have her.”
</DeathVoice>

Elana used the sword of her father and <span class="fx-slash">cut across the emperor’s back</span>. In the place where his heart stood, now the sword pierced his body. He trembled in agony.

“Impossible,” he said as the life of his eyes vanished. “A god can’t die.”

<DeathVoice>
  “<span class="fx-key">Even gods must die,</span>”
</DeathVoice>

said Odeia <span class="fx-slash">cutting his thread</span> for his soul wasn’t worthy of even the lowest existence. The mistcrawlers and souls extinguished with him. Their essence went to the skies and disappeared, breaking in sparks and light. Odeia caught the divine essence before it was lost. She absorbed it all.

<MoodShift theme="ash" label="OBLIVION" />

The corpse fell to the ground, <span class="fx-ghost">turning to dust.</span> He was older than a thousand years and time was keeping up with his body. No bells rang for him, no chorus or orchestra. The man was deaf in a world of music, and he couldn’t stop shouting.

He was sentenced to something worse than wandering the world, something she knew he would despise. <span class="fx-key">Oblivion.</span>

In the end of days, there was nothing left of him, <span class="fx-ghost">not even his name.</span>
