<span class="fx-stretch">Time stood still</span> and the pass of days blurred in her eyes. There was nothing constricting her to the world anymore and <span class="fx-ghost">months and years were gone like sand in her hands.</span>

The ash continued to fall, the Sun became darker and colder, the lands were flattening.

She could not calculate the pass of time with accuracy, her perception was stretched. In the blink of an eye, <span class="fx-stretch">millennia were gone.</span> Then another, then more. Then the Sun extinguished and the last form of knowing time died with it. Days were no more, only perpetual nights. Cold nights. Odeia was happy that she was alone, no being should suffer said conditions.

At first, she spent her days with her violin. Playing and humming beside a window like she used to do. Soon she realized that a concert without an audience was equivalent to silence. She stopped playing after a few thousand years.

Then she wandered the world again. Looking at the landscapes that were forgotten, places that could not sustain life anymore.

After enough time, she ran out of places to explore. The boredom that she hated began to return.

*I’m tired*, she thought and got surprised. How long had it been since she had a thought?

She couldn’t tell.

Once there was nothing to explore, she turned to the books that Elana used to read. Stories and tales of men. History of war and blood. She didn’t like those kinds of stories, she preferred the ones with the happy endings. But life was never happy. She found books that she liked, those dedicated to children. Going through them all, not a single one was left. And she got bored again.

She felt like distances were changing. The buildings and fields seemed smaller—compacted. Maybe her movement became faster, maybe the world was shrinking. Enveloped by darkness, it was hard to tell.

Odeia learned about her love for stories. But there were no more stories to tell, she had read them all. *Smart*, she thought. She would create her own stories, now she had a limitless amount of entertainment. She could see the world end before the stories she had inside of her were finished.

A boring life was nothing but expected. Still, Odeia took it with hope. A wide smile and a hand on her heart. She had to continue, carrying the sound of the world with her.

<span class="fx-stretch">Silence.</span>

The universe blended together in a mesh of monotonous mass. A messy tangle of threads and strings that the goddess could play. There was no one there to hear it, but she wanted to remember the forgotten ones. She played the song of existence, the sound of a withered world, the melody of the end of times. The music was gloomy, but silence was worse.

She knew that the sad tones came from the dull place. Odeia then forced the sound to be as the ones she had heard. <span class="fx-chime">The orchestras of the pure souls</span> that dwelled on the world. She played them together—all at once—and felt the vibrations.

The sounds worked like one, like a <span class="fx-key">single symphony made to last forever</span>.

It tasted like pie and smelled like flowers. It was strong and fragile at the same time. It smiled like an innocent girl. It was beautiful. The combination of all the souls she had deemed worthy.

<Silence height="md" ornament="hollow" />

And then came the silence again.

She became the universe. Odeia had taken the divine essence a long time ago, but she never used the power. Power of the soul—one to create anything. Her will was truth, and what she spoke was reality. She had vibrated with the universe at the highest level, her physical body disappeared. Now time and space were one with her. Her conscience permeated all there was.

Chaos became from it.

She looked at eternity and it looked back. She had the power to create and destroy alike, she could rule like the old gods did. Only a thought was necessary and she would have brought the world to its original state, but she knew better. Odeia could see time as one, not a straight line of events, but a cycle of eternal return. She saw the worlds come to be and get destroyed, always by the gods who had created them in the first place.

The divine essence was taken endless times and endless gods appeared. They all died for they had been greedy with their intentions. The gods were the center of their universe, forcing praise by wars and blood. Eventually someone would rise and kill them. Take the power. Start again.

The cycle was infinite.

Odeia didn’t want the power, she came to it by accident. The only thing she desired was for music to be played, and people to dance at it. She knew what she had to do.

<span class="fx-cleave fx-key">The cycle broke.</span>

<MoodShift theme="bone" label="LIGHT" />

<span class="fx-whisper">*Light*,</span> Odeia thought. And light came to be. But there was nothing to be lit other than herself.

She had a body, but not the one the Sun gave her. Her hair was longer, her organs regenerated from the void. She looked like a human now, one with four arms and four meters tall, but human nonetheless. Her appearance was familiar to her. Memories of past lives came to her, the ones she had forgotten.

She felt peace in her heart.

*We need a place to walk*, she thought and the first world was created—nothing like the one she had lived in. Not a torus this time, she was tired of self-repeating worlds. She felt like the best shape would be a sphere. With the power of Atorus, she held it on the skies and made many others to accompany it.

Not a single word, but a universe of them. They all <span data-action="surge">came from her, <span class="fx-impact">like an explosion</span></span>. Going until the end of her reach.

Now the worlds existed but they were dark and the skies were empty. *We need a sun and stars*, she thought. But the ways the old gods made things weren’t to her liking. She wanted a world that could exist without the divine. One that didn’t need her. Odeia would not make the mistake to force the universe revolve around the Sun—or her for that matter—so <span class="fx-key">she created an infinity of them, one to light every group of planets.</span> They looked like stars in the distance.

If a world ended, there were many others to replace it.

She would never have to worry about repeating the mistakes of the past. She had learned from the mistakes of the other gods.

She filled the space with wonders, things that would color the dark nights. She understood the passion that Elana had while painting the walls of the palace. She did the same, decorating each corner.

Once everything was created, she went down to one of her favorite planets. Odeia stood on a hill—an empty one.

*There is only one thing left*, she thought.

From her bag she took out the flower—the one Elana gave her back—and blew gently on it. From it came thousands of seeds that <span data-action="fall">landed on the ground, coloring everything they touched</span>. Giving the place some life.

Exhausted, Odeia lied on the ground. Everything was beautiful again, maybe even more than it ever was. She was happy with her creation. She had made the right choice.

Time was not a circle anymore; the world didn’t revolve around the gods. She liked what she created but it still felt too silent.

The most important thing was left for the end. Life was yet to be created. The goddess took her violin and <span class="fx-chime">played a piece for the flowers.</span> They danced with her, flying in circles. A swirl formed with the music and from it formed the first human.

The girl looked up and smiled. “Good morning,” she answered.

Odeia soon renounced her power and it went to the skies. It flew away with the wind and covered the world. It shot to the stars and traveled the entire existence. Where essence fell, something was born. Life filled the universe like no other had seen.

Odeia could finally rest. She laid on that field for so long that plants grew around her. The early humans came to her and gave her tribute, they praised her. She never liked that kind of things. At the beginning she told them stories about the earlier worlds, sharing her knowledge. But she could never stop them from thinking of her as a god. She knew what she had to do; she was needed no more.

<span class="fx-whisper">*Everyone has to die*,</span> she thought as she closed her eyes and submerged herself in a deep sleep. In the hill where she gave the breath of life, she was buried by time and <span class="fx-ghost">slowly forgotten.</span> The flowers nearby surrounded her so she would never be alone.

<span class="fx-key">And something new began—a place for the little things.</span>
