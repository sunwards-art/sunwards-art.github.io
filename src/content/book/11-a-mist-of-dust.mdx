She offered to take the girl on her arms—just as she did the day before. Elana shook her head denying the offer. Ruppert wouldn’t say no, he grew eager to travel in arms, and only would walk a few hours a day.

The landscape looked far less gloomy than it did at night. It was clear that it used to be full of vegetation. Now everything was dry. The Sun shone faintly in the sky, letting the dead weeds be seen. Odeia remembered the landscapes before the unlit days, full of the color that the world had lost.

*I wonder if she can imagine it*, she thought. *It would be very hard to imagine something that differed so much from her reality.*

They continued with a fast pace. If they wanted to rest at night, they would have to move without pause during the day. Their next stop would be at least half a day of travel from there.

The old city peeked in the distance.

Odeia knew this place. She had been there but she didn’t remember it. Trying to recall details about it, however, made her twist in disgust.

<DeathVoice>
  “Deilos. That is the name of the city.”
</DeathVoice>

She struggled to remember more.

A large port city located in the commercial heart of the Empire. It was noon already when they reached the gates. The Sun was not leaking anymore. The gates were wide and tall, made of stone and led to a broken bridge. Lucky of them, the bridge was useless as the rivers and oceans were long gone. They passed through the cyclopean architecture of Deilos, city of Oldraan. The buildings looked out of place. Columns and walls over each other, covering the foundation of old stone.

The goddess felt the sand on her feet, cold and dry. She missed the water, she used to like it. They walked under the fallen bridge and crossed to the other side. They were in Deilos.

Another twist in the gut. Heart pumping faster. *Why does this place make me feel like this?* she thought. The same eerie feeling she had when she encountered the ocean of souls.

The wide streets of the city were enough to fit big groups of people shopping and trading, but where the crowds of travelers made the city feel alive only bodies were left. These weren’t normal bodies, they had expressions of sorrow and pain. They were different, though, to the carbonized corpses that Elana destroyed on her way. These ones weren’t black and covered by soot, they were gray. From them, particles of themselves fell off to the ground like ashes—covering the entire floor. Flasks and bottles laid on the floor, tainted by blood. They moved past the corpses on the ground, <span class="fx-ghost">consuming themselves slowly until they were dust.</span> The whole city was covered by this dust, dense enough to make it hard to breathe for a normal person.

“What happened to these people,” Elana said masking her face with her clothes.

<DeathVoice>
  “Not my doing.”
</DeathVoice>

“Don’t you know anything about this? This seems similar to the mist.”

<DeathVoice>
  “They are related, somehow. I wonder how they died for their bodies to be like this. I worry for their souls.”
</DeathVoice>

She couldn’t tell if this was the result of a war of some kind or the chaos of the unlit days. She didn’t pay attention to the souls she took in those days and now she regretted it. Death became curious of how these humans lived before the end and tried to remember more about them.

At the end of the street, they got in front of a big statue of some king. His name was not visible on the inscription for tear and time had erased it. Behind it was a cemetery of ships—deep buried on the sand—where the ocean left its absence. Odeia tried to feel the breeze but it was gone, only the taste of salt remained. They continued along the beach, feeling the cold sand between her toes, walking slowly and thinking about the past. At her left, the ships reminded her of younger ages, times of wealth and abundance. At her right, the biggest market of Oldraan now stood abandoned.

They stopped along the way.

“This place looks so different to what we’ve seen before,” Elana said.

<DeathVoice>
  “This is the Empire. The real Empire. They took places like this for their own.”
</DeathVoice>

The architecture was very specific to the place. It was much older than Oldraan or any kingdom who reigned before it. Death saw the memories in the piled stones and heard their stories. The peaceful people of Deilos, called skaa by the original tribes, lived there since the beginning of humanity, at least until the expansion of the kingdom started. The colonizers found great value in there for the river that crossed the city connecting the continent from west to east. This made the port an important place for commerce and travel. They fought for it. A one-sided war that could hold only one victor, Oldraan absorbed the territories and killed the ones who opposed it. Anahu', king of the skaa died by execution and with him his people slowly disappeared. The once sacred lands now belonged to the emperor.

*I have a bad feeling about this*, Odeia thought.

She could remember some more. The old city was one of the biggest maritime ports during and after the Sunrise era. Merchants of all over the world gathered there and they sold their products for the entire east to enjoy. Anything could be found on the market if you knew the right people and had enough money. In the old age, it was normal to see its streets full of people and sellers, the constant crowds meant an endless noise. Some people called Deilos the city that never slept.

Death stopped walking as she finally knew where she stood.

<DeathVoice>
  “Damned be the day.”
</DeathVoice>

Seven people in front of her fought and the seven of them died. An almost empty flask was left in the floor with a small drop of water still at the bottom. <span class="fx-key">They had fought for it and perished by greed and ignorance.</span> She hated this city and its people alike. Even glad she was of its decaying state as in her mind they deserved what they got.

Death finally remembered this place. The deep blue sea that touched the coasts of Deilos and the calm waters made the city a common vacation spot. Kings and dukes went there to spend a few weeks of rest. Surrounded by vineyards on the low hills and full of hotels and hostels, this represented an important income for the kingdom. Of course, the most important attraction of them all was the curative waters. From the springs emerged a constant flow of a glowing green water that was said to have healing abilities. Everyone who traveled east had to go through this port so it was a very busy place for travelers and thieves alike, but the healing water of Deilos was specially reserved for those who could afford it.

<DeathVoice>
  “This city is cursed. We must not stay for long in here.”
</DeathVoice>

“What happened in here?” Elana asked.

<DeathVoice>
  “They condemned themselves. It’s better if we just find our way out.”
</DeathVoice>

She pointed at the vials.

<DeathVoice>
  “You see that? They died for it. If you see one, keep your hand for yourself.”
</DeathVoice>

Elana frowned and nodded.

The healing practices were handled by the church of Sunlight. They had complete power over the water sources and they only answered to the king. All of this was used as a political advance often. The church was judge and executioner when it came about who could use the waters. The adepts had to wait for weeks at the doors of the Cathedral to even have an audience and explain their ailment. Of course, the powerful and famous had a preference in the matter. Corruption in Deilos was in the mouth of everyone who spoke about it, the black market offered flasks and baths for expensive amounts and only wealthy merchants could afford it.

The magic of the city was real. The people of the area lived longer and better than anywhere else. Just a touch was enough to take away any pain; if you drank it, it healed any illness; topic application got rid of scars and wounds; full submersion gave you a few weeks more of life; and its taste was said to be as if gold was drinkable. The kings, who came every year, lived past 300 years only surpassed by the high priests who outlived any king.

Death used to hate the place as much as she hated any form of elongating life. <span class="fx-key">The threads of life had a determined time and if you extended it, it could get stretched and distorted.</span> Even worse, a thread could get tangled and become harmful for others. The sound of a thread that was longer than supposed to was muted and dirty. She despised that.

*Why would they die over some water*, Elana thought. Water was scarce, true, but it wasn’t as difficult to get for them to kill each other.

*Keep your hands to yourself.* The words of the goddess lingered on her mind. *It’s better to believe her.*

*When have I lied to you before?* Her father's voice resounded on her head and she frowned. *Liar*, she thought.

If she couldn’t trust her own father, how could she trust anyone else.

“What did the water do?” she asked.

<DeathVoice>
  “It would be better for you if you just don’t touch it.”
</DeathVoice>

*Liar*, she thought again. If they had died fighting for it, it had to possess some kind of power. A power that she didn’t want her to have.

She skimmed over the flasks, looking for one with the liquid still inside. If there was some left—which would be difficult judging by how desperate they looked—she would take some for herself.

*Believe me*, the voice of Odeia said. *No*, Elana thought. *I won’t.*

*Peace*, Odeia thought. There was no one left in the city and the newfound silence was to her liking.

<MoodShift theme="dusk" label="THE CATHEDRAL OF SUNLIGHT" />

They left the beach behind and walked the main street that led to the Cathedral of the Church of Sunlight. It was taller than the rest of the buildings, atop the hill in the middle of town. With tall columns and stone walls, it had golden details all over it. It seemed to pierce the sky with its spiky towers. It conserved its imposing and grandiose image, even beautiful in its own way. Once, it gathered all citizens at its gates to pray and ask for mercy. Each of them was forced to attend there once a week and pay the tribute of protection—which was voluntary at first but ended as a law. The entire Empire enforced these laws.

Elana admired the gothic architecture. Her jaw dropped.

The rose window in the front had an image of the sun. Not the usual and simplified symbol of a star, but his humanoid form, holding his shield and sword.

“Is that him?” Elana asked.

<DeathVoice>
  “He is. That’s how he looks or, at least, how I remember him.”
</DeathVoice>

The portals were taller than her, doors were wide open, something she never saw before in all the years she had come. It was more clear than ever that the church was gone. The inside was well kept, a hall filled with paintings of gods, with ponds and bathtubs, all of them dry. Death looked at the murals trying to find herself, but she wasn’t there. No one had ever adored her.

In the middle of the hall stood a fountain, as dry as the ocean outside. Death walked past behind it. There was a corridor that led to a throne. It had the symbols of the Sun everywhere and it was golden but covered by rust. A corpse was still sitting there.

She didn’t remember it but she had come already to take this life. This upset her.

<DeathVoice>
  “I hope that the suffering you’ve brought to the people in this place you had to endure for yourself. If not, I’ll personally precede for your eternal suffering as no penalty in life could be enough.”
</DeathVoice>

She pulled the corpse of the high priest to the ground.

<DeathVoice>
  <span class="fx-ritual">“There’s no place on a throne for a twisted soul like you.</span> <span class="fx-key">I curse the day my scissors touched your impure thread.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

Death used her clothes to clean her scissors and walked away. She was visibly displeased and her emotions, still volatile as she was not yet accustomed to them, broke into a rage. A rage that compelled her to destroy. She first punched the wall of the gods lacking her presence. Not satisfied she came back and folded the throne and twisted it to resemble the ones who used it. Then with her four arms she <span class="fx-cleave">pulled the columns, teared the walls and destroyed the ponds</span>. Pieces of wood and stone flying everywhere, <span class="fx-impact">the entire building was trembling</span>. From the coasts the shudders could be heard. In a matter of seconds, <span class="fx-impact">the whole structure was down</span>. A mountain of rubble rose fast and soon settled in. <span class="fx-whisper">The city went quiet again.</span>

<span class="fx-key">This was the rage of a wronged goddess.</span>

In the end, the riches and power meant nothing. <span class="fx-key">They were all dust on the ground and no water could clean the dirt they had become.</span>
