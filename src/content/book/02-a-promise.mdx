Death had been walking aimlessly when she noticed her body moving on its own. Her last memory was the sadness of a flower and the beautiful sound it made. Again, she had lost her mind in the mud, losing control over time. It could have been a few days since the last cutting, but Death couldn’t tell. Her perception of time had <span class="fx-stretch">contracted again</span>—something that happened frequently now that she was alone. Her mind started to wonder and so did her body. Moving without thought.

In the past she had seen many beautiful things. The garden of an old woman, the wishes of a dying seeker, the last kiss of a widow, the unlikely survival of a delicate flower; she wondered if at least one of these things could survive on such conditions.

Her thoughts were interrupted abruptly. It was a <span class="fx-chime">jingle, a sweet sound of a bell.</span> She looked nervously at her necklace. It was shining in light and sound. With a smirk, she was excited to see something alive. Sadly, it would not last for long and she would have to cut its thread. She hated herself for loving the sound.

On her busy days, she never saw the cease of life as something bad. It was just a part of life itself, the beginning of something greater. After the cutting, the souls left the physical world and ascended to the skies. There, they would spend the rest of their time dancing with the Moon. But no moon was there to dance, and the skies shined in darkness. Death didn’t know what happened to the souls after being cut and that troubled her mind.

With torpor, she moved to the closest building. Among the loose bricks and shattered columns, she found a corner in good state. <span class="fx-cleave">Four arms on the angle, quick tear, big hole.</span> A connection between the two places was created in an instance and Death stepped in.

In another place, far away, where the snowy mountains and foggy valleys became arid deserts, a cloud of dust appeared. In a village that lost its name when all its people perished, Death took form. The insides of the building were of wooden floors and paper walls. A place oddly conserved considering what had happened to the zone. The sound of the bell changed. <span class="fx-chime">It was a chorus now,</span> it guided her to the next room.

*I will be damned*, she thought when she peeked inside.

The room looked old, but most of it was still intact—as if nothing bad had ever happened in there. The walls were decorated with paintings and portraits, there were ornaments everywhere. It looked like a religious place, but not to the gods that Death knew, different gods from other places; and for all she had known, just as dead. The room stood still but in a dark corner something was moving.

The goddess heard a meow. A mother cat with three kittens—all of them as black as night—calling for help. It had been a few months since she saw a cat, she had thought that the creatures were extinct. The older cat looked too thin to maintain herself, let alone feed the others. They were meowing to their mother but the sound was almost mute. All of them were as frail as the thread that came from them and raised to the sky. They made a melody that echoed in unison in the room.

*Poor creatures*, she thought. *There’s nothing I can do now.*

<DeathVoice>
  “I know how hard it’s been. You’ve been brave for your kittens and protected your kin even more than your own life.”
</DeathVoice>

Death petted the mother but she barely responded to her touch. The kittens were almost immobile—all of them with closed eyes. She tried to wake them up, but they were hungry and tired. She talked to them and made different noises, she took them and tried to offer them water. The cats did not respond, this was but a postponing of what would come to happen. The time had come for them, and she knew it.

She touched the silver threads and <span class="fx-key">the sound coming from them resembled a string quartet.</span> A rich melody formed from all the threads combined. A death like this was not common at all.

The four creatures acknowledged Death’s presence. The mother nodded to her, as if accepting her fate. She let out a faint noise, a goodbye to life. They were ready.

<DeathVoice>
  <span class="fx-ritual">“Damned will be the world that feels your absence. Sad will be the day after you’ve left. Take this not as a curse, but as relief of your grief. May the rest you lacked on matter you find in essence. Your life has come to an end, and it is my duty to lift you from this world. I wish you all a sweet dream.”</span>
</DeathVoice>

Death petted them some more, trying to let them know that it would be fine. She positioned them together, all by their mom and let them rest. A rest they’ll have forever.

<DeathVoice>
  “This will not hurt. Nothing will hurt you anymore. <span class="fx-whisper">Good night.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

Scissors in hand, she grabbed all their threads together. With a <span class="fx-slash">quick snip</span> their souls were cut from this world. A tear shed down her face, she smudged it with her clothes. The soundless meows and the chorus of strings stopped altogether. The room stood quiet.

Once she calmed and was about to leave the room, she looked back. The corpses laid on the ground motionless, still warm.

*I hate this*, she thought.

Hate was—for practical purposes—useless, that used to be her thought while observing humans. They had stained their hands for vengeance and envy, always letting their emotions drive their actions. She didn’t use to feel hate, but times were different, she had changed.

Death took a last look before stepping out, but something made her stop cold. A meow. The desperate sound came from the same room. She started to search where it came from but couldn’t find anything. With the bell in her hand, she tried to make sure that she didn’t make a mistake leaving a cat still alive. The bell was silent.

There was something in that room and it was alive. Death started to lift the furniture and tear the walls but the kitten did not appear anywhere. Another meow, this time a harder one, like it was clinging to life and asking to be found. Death put her ear on the floor, it came from below it. She <span class="fx-cleave">ripped the floor open</span>, throwing the wood out of the way.

<MoodShift theme="dusk" label="A SMALL LIFE BENEATH THE FLOOR" />

In there, a black kitten was trying to find his way out. He was small—smaller even than his brothers—but he was the only one remaining. He tried to climb, but his weakened limbs couldn’t hold his weight. He had used all his energy to call for help. The kitten was hungry. She picked him up and took him to her chest. She felt the faint pulse and soft breath. He was still alive. Death hugged him, wrapping him on her cloak. She took the paper walls and wood she had teared; in the center of the room, she made a fire.

She put him close to the flames so that he got warm. Out of the building, she searched for water and something to eat. She used all her speed. Usually, she held back on her movements, because there was no rush. Now she had a reason, she had to save that cat. Back on the room, she gave him some water and he drank desperately. After he had calmed and quenched his thirst, he found his way onto Death’s lap. He was slow, but even when his body was weak, she sensed great strength coming from him. Getting lost in the blackness of her cloth, he got up slowly. At night, with not much light, only his eyes were visible. Green eyes, tired eyes, closed eyes. He fell asleep over her. She didn’t move to avoid bothering him.

*I want to touch it*, she thought.

She saw how humans had done it. They shared a bond with some animals and they expressed their affection rubbing each other. Death didn’t have skin to feel, but something compelled her to do it. Something deep inside told her that she should do it.

Gently, she put a hand over him. He didn’t flinch, already in a profound sleep. <span class="fx-key">She had a life in her hands. So fragile yet beautiful.</span> Maybe beauty came from its short course of life? From their brittle existence. He was breathing, slowly but steadily. His eyes weren’t fully closed and his mouth was barely open, twinkling with the light. His fur shined with the fire as she started to pet him.

From the list of all lesser beings, humans were her less favorites. Other animals had always some charm to them, she liked to spend her scarce free time looking at them.

He was soft, extremely soft. She couldn’t know by touch, but she could tell by the look of it. It seemed like he enjoyed it, because he stretched his entire body on her lap—relaxing completely—with all extremities extended. She couldn’t move, not without waking him up.

Death remained motionless for the night.

<Silence height="sm" ornament="dot" />

After a good night, the cat woke up. The small animal saw a dark figure in front of him. It was so tall that it had to bend to fit with the ceiling. He had never seen something as terrifying, but he was no fool, he knew that it had saved him and was grateful for it. He rubbed his head on Death’s chest as a sign of gratitude. She petted him back as his purring started.

<DeathVoice>
  “It is very fortunate that you have survived. It would have been a disgrace for the Moon to lose you.”
</DeathVoice>

The cat didn’t bother to look at her and continued to rub in her. He then climbed her cloak until he reached her arms. She ended up petting him.

She put him back on the floor and stepped away.

<DeathVoice>
  “As long as you’re alive I’ll carry with my duty, but our paths depart in different ways. It’s an honor to be in the presence of such majestic creature. I hope for the magic you carry to carry on. So long, lone traveler, until the end of your days.”
</DeathVoice>

She was leaving the room, but he went after her. She had saved him and wanted to give back.

<DeathVoice>
  “How come you’re still nearby? Didn’t you hear me? This is our farewell; we have to separate—”
</DeathVoice>

The cat started to headbutt her in the ankles. He rubbed his cheeks, marking her as his. They were bonded now and nothing could break their connection.

<Silence height="sm" ornament="dot" />

Death was on the corridor, a window stood behind her. Without looking back, she jumped through it—back first—and grabbed to the walls. Just by standing straight, she could reach the dark tile roof on top. She climbed and sat on the edge. Sun was going down. The few wooden houses around got the last lights on them. The town shined with red, and purple shadows formed pointing to south. What used to be rice fields on the distance, blackened first. The light left the roof soon after. The Sun disappeared.

<DeathVoice>
  “What a curious animal. But there’s no way that we could travel together. I have a duty and no distraction can get in the way. It's the way of—”
</DeathVoice>

The sound of something small approaching from below distracted her mid-sentence. Struggling to fit in a hole on the roof, the small cat had gotten up there. He pushed through and moved towards her, then proceeded to rub against her again. She couldn’t help but pet him.

His warmth was lost on her, she knew it was there, but couldn’t experience it. His presence was, however, to her liking, but she knew that Death and life do not bond. They are meant to be opposites.

*It’s the way of things*, she thought.

<DeathVoice>
  “You have determination, I give you that, but your place is here among the living. I don’t know where my next cutting will take me and I can’t care for a form of life so fragile, so… beautiful. I guess you’re truly beautiful, and even when I can’t sense your touch, I feel good about it. I’m glad for your existence but our ways drift apart.”
</DeathVoice>

*He was the sign that I’ve been looking for*, the goddess thought. *The hope I had lost. <span class="fx-key">A prospect of beauty for this blind world.</span>*

The cat was irresponsive to her words, he only kept rubbing against her.

<DeathVoice>
  “But of course, you don’t care about all that, you just want what feels right, you want warmth. Something I can’t provide. I can’t blame you, though; I would do the same in your position.”
</DeathVoice>

She looked at the landscape around her.

<DeathVoice>
  “To live in these conditions will be impossible for you.”
</DeathVoice>

She sighed.

<DeathVoice>
  “I was the one who saved you, was not I? Now I feel responsible for what happens with you. I’m compelled to keep you—I don’t want to lie—but this is unheard of. If I leave you here, it would be the same than sentence you to die. I’m not the one to decide that. Your thread is strong and with many years to go. You should live.”
</DeathVoice>

*A goddess with a pet?* she thought. *It’s curious that the less humans there were, the more like one she was becoming. I have to be consequent with my acts. I must watch out for the poor thing.*

She stepped up and raised him to have a better look. His eyes were too sweet for this world. Too pure.

<DeathVoice>
  “No harm will come to you. That much I promise. <span class="fx-key">We will travel together from now on, until the end of your days.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

After a final pat on the head, she used some of her cloth to wrap him and <span data-action="fall">jumped off the roof</span>. The cat remained calm on her arms and even fell asleep while she moved. They got lost on the horizon.

They started to travel together. The bell sounded a few more times, some rats and insects and other smaller beings died, but Death didn’t find something as interesting as her new companion. They enjoyed each other’s company, traveling every corner of the world to seek the dying souls. He seemed to like meeting new places and was very curious of his surroundings.

Days and weeks passed for them and now Death felt time more real than ever. She saw the cat grow from a weak kitten to a strong adult and that kept her grounded in this world. She noticed though that he grew up slower than what she thought was normal. Like time had slowed down for the world. Still, she remembered every second since she found him and every soul was present in her mind. Her essential form was becoming more in touch with matter.

The cat became attached to her and apparently experienced the same magic that she saw filled the world. His ears reacted to the sound of the threads and he could see it rising to the skies. They were sharing more than simple company.

Death didn’t wander aimlessly anymore and only moved when it was necessary. Her mind was clear, her thoughts straight. Death found new purpose in life.

After one of their cuttings, they found themselves on Death Port. They had been there before multiple times and he had come to like the place. The island was a common stop for fishermen, so it was filled with food and drinks. It was known by the rum they made, a rum so strong that you could taste it for hours and feel it for weeks. Sailors from East to West came and drank as a ritual before the fishing. They always left with enough barrels of the stuff to die from intoxication, but they always returned.

The only town that stood had a heavy smell of alcohol. The street was flooded and the cat enjoyed to jump on every puddle. They had been there for a long time and Death started to inquire when the next soul would come, but no soul came and her bell remained in silence. After a week she thought the world to be over. The bell never stood quiet for quite as long. A few hours, maybe days, but never that long. <span class="fx-ghost">The world was fading before her eyes,</span> the end was nigh.

<MoodShift theme="ash" label="ONLY THREE THREADS REMAIN" />

She approached a cliff where once the sight of the sea was delightful for the lucky eyes that saw it. The rocks were sharp so she put the cat on her lap while sitting on the edge. A tall black cliff with shiny rocks, and at the bottom only sand.

<DeathVoice>
  “We may be the last of what is left. No more gods, no more men, only us,”
</DeathVoice>

the cat slept while she talked.

<DeathVoice>
  “I’ve been asking myself if there’s anyone left out there, and I have to know. I’m just as curious as you are.”
</DeathVoice>

Death had a bag always with her. Made of the Moon’s veil, the bag was timeless and endless. She could keep anything that could fit through indefinitely. From there she took out a small scroll. This was the old list of threads. She had never used it before, too busy with the call of the bell to even have a look. It showed the souls soon to be cut, but the scroll was never as light. After seeing inside, she knew why the bell didn’t ring anymore. <span class="fx-key">Only three threads remained and one of them looked thin and frail.</span>

<span class="fx-whisper">The bell rang again.</span>
