“Then, how old are you?” asked Elana, they had been talking all the way and she got interested in her.

<DeathVoice>
  “I do not count the years like you humans do. I am as old as life is, minus the few years the first being lived.”
</DeathVoice>

“Does that mean you’ve seen the last moments of everyone?”

<DeathVoice>
  “Only the ones who have a soul. No gods, no giants, no titans. Every living being who died of natural causes.”
</DeathVoice>

“Natural causes? I don’t understand.”

<DeathVoice>
  “If you die by old age or sickness, I will be there. On the other hand, if you die by the hand of man or your own doing, then I will not.”
</DeathVoice>

“What happens to those who die like that?”

<DeathVoice>
  “In such cases, their souls do not abandon this world. They are probably still around. They will never ascend to the skies.”
</DeathVoice>

“Isn’t that better than a normal death?”

<DeathVoice>
  “Of course not, their souls start fading with time. <span class="fx-ghost">Their essence vanishes</span> until only their desires are left, wandering around aimlessly. <span class="fx-ghost">They forgot who they were, and everyone forgot them.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

“What a horrible fate.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Indeed...”
</DeathVoice>

They were too concentrated on their talk to notice that they were approaching a big structure made of rock. The path led to the entrance and continued underground. Most of the building was buried and it had no windows. The door was a rock four times bigger than Death who was already at least twice the height of Elana. They touched the entrance but with the enormous size, it didn’t budge. The goddess was strong but lacked the force that it required to open it. They tried to force themselves inside, but their efforts were hopeless.

“How will we enter?” asked the girl. “Maybe we can go around it, the path probably continues on the other side.”

As soon as she stepped outside of the reddish sand, her foot started to sink. In a blink her whole leg was swallowed by the desert. Death assisted the leg and lifted her completely over the sand.

<DeathVoice>
  “You cannot step outside the path, otherwise you’ll be eaten. This is a trap of Atorus, the world, he wanted to keep something from escaping.”
</DeathVoice>

“Can this be the place where the giants live? My father told me the stories.”

<DeathVoice>
  “There is a possibility. They weren’t sons of the moon, so they didn’t have a soul. I’m yet to see the insides of that fortress. We could even find some giants still around.”
</DeathVoice>

Elana looked at the big entrance again. She spotted an engraving on the top.

“Can you read what that says?”

<DeathVoice>
  “It must not be so hard. Some of it seems erased by time, but let me have a better look.”
</DeathVoice>

She climbed the door and shook off the sand. The words read: <span class="fx-key">“Keep away those who travel with the Sun. Only the ones who live in shadow may enter.”</span>

None of that made sense to Death, Elana did not understand much either. They tried to find some hidden meaning on those words, but nothing came to their minds. After a while thinking, they both gave up.

“Maybe it’s nothing. Something to scare away travelers.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Oh, believe me child. Nothing is ever only nothing, there must be a reason for it. I just cannot think of one.”
</DeathVoice>

After a while they both gave up trying to understand the engraving. Elana was out of ideas on how to progress and with the upcoming night, she decided to set everything to sleep.

Elana made a quick fire with most of the sticks she had left. She leaned on the door and fell asleep quickly. The cat looked for warmth in her, he preferred her company rather than Death’s. What was left of the Sun settled down and the only light came from the barely lit fire. With so few sticks, it didn’t take much time to burn down. Soon they were enveloped in shadows.

<MoodShift theme="void" label="ONLY THE SHADOWS ENTER" />

The stone entrance began to move. The cat was the first one to notice, waking up Elana in the process. He ran towards Death for protection. She stood up and put Elana behind her. They couldn’t see much, if it weren’t for the sounds and <span class="fx-impact">trembling of the earth</span>, they wouldn’t have known what was happening. <span class="fx-cleave">The whole door was being eaten by the sand,</span> in a matter of seconds it was gone. The opening was clear for the travelers.

Elana used some oils from her bag, with a stick and a cloth she made a torch. It didn’t let her see further than a few feet in front of her but just enough to walk. Death guided the advance as she didn’t need light to see. The group descended without sharing a word first.

The path led to stairs, <span class="fx-stretch">countless stairs</span> that went deep below the surface. The walls were tall and solid, made of stone. Stones that should have been brought from far away as there was nowhere near that they could come from. The dim light of the improvised torch showed drawings decorating the way. Beautiful art, long lost skills. Elana was amazed by the shapes and forms the pictures showed, she had never seen such colors before.

“What is all this?” asked the girl.

<DeathVoice>
  “This must be the art of the giants. It was known by very few the mastery they had in the arts. They were scholars.”
</DeathVoice>

“But my father told me they were a warrior tribe.”

<DeathVoice>
  “A tribe? Your father was very mistaken on that. They were no tribe; they were more advanced than most human kingdoms. They did have a reputation for their great strength, but with that size it was only reasonable to expect so. Descendants of the gods, they were blessed with their knowledge. Without a soul, they were immortal.”
</DeathVoice>

They had reached a door. Death had to push it hard to open it.

<DeathVoice>
  “Welcome to the kingdom of giants and home of Almir.”
</DeathVoice>

<MoodShift theme="ash" label="CHAMBERS OF MAGMA AND STEEL" />

The door led to a gigantic structure inside, they were standing at its top. They had a minimum sight of the bottom; it was too far away. Columns extended for miles connecting floor and ceiling. Paths and bridges connected everything, holding together with heavy chains, looking like the webs spiders used to make. Everything lit by magma, a faint and slowly moving magma.

“This place looks abandoned.”

Elana put the torch away. The magma slid down from the walls providing enough light to see with her bare eyes. They walked the bridge until they got to the next land of mass, an enormous hall welcomed them. More and more ornaments hanged on the walls, dressed with murals and paintings, and on the middle of the hall, a big statue stood. It was silver and radiated the light it reflected. With a triumphant pose, at its base it read: “Imposing Almir, liberator of his kin.”

“This must be Almir, the dreadful. He does look like a great warrior. It is said that he cannot be beaten in fight.”

<DeathVoice>
  “No one remains undefeated, not even immortals. If the Sun could be taken down then anyone can. Walk without any fear.”
</DeathVoice>

“Does that mean you can also lose in a fight?”

<DeathVoice>
  “How do you think that I lost my hand? It was a human who cut it. A relentless human, even when badly injured, he gave a great fight.”
</DeathVoice>

“How can humans fight gods? Their powers are not even comprehensible for me.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Don’t understate your own power. <span class="fx-key">Humans were capable of great deeds, things the gods feared.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

“Is that why you take the life of men?”

Odeia didn’t have an answer, she stopped cold to think. Looking at the statue of the great giant, she said:

<DeathVoice>
  “That could be the reason. Maybe they feared their creation.”
</DeathVoice>

They abandoned the hall, following the bridge that took them to the next mass of land. They kept walking, but the way only opened in more paths and bifurcations. They tried to stay on their way to the north; Elana used her compass to do so. They went up and down, left, then right; they were lost. Then they saw more weapons, armors and skeletons. They were an entire army.

“Maybe we should follow the corpses,” said Elana.

<DeathVoice>
  “It is never a good idea to follow the corpses. I’d advise to stay away from them.”
</DeathVoice>

“But they could have known where to go in this place. Let’s see where they take us. We won’t find an exit otherwise.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Agreed. I will trust in your good judgment,”
</DeathVoice>

answered Odeia.

They changed their course. Not following what the needle marked to be the north but the mound of piling corpses. Instead of walking their way up, they started to descend even more. The number of bodies only grew as they advanced, but then they found not only the leftovers of men. Giants, dead and rotten giants. Their skulls were the size of a human arm; their jaws could tear apart any warrior. They wore heavy armors, protected to the bones, using maces and hammers as their weapons. The shields were folded like paper, no material could support a strike from one of those beasts.

They saw smaller giants, maybe they were only children. It was a massacre. They had fought with great braveness, but the endless number of men surpassed them by the thousands. For each giant they found, at least a hundred men had died fighting it, even with the smaller ones. Half-done paintings, food not eaten. They had attacked by surprise.

They made their way into a fortress. A sphere made with rock, hanging in the middle of the air, anchoring it with thick chains of pure steel. Multiple bridges connected to it, like it was the center of the gigantic structure. The door was broken, the soldiers had entered. A throne was there, with its king still sitting. The corpses were obliterated around it, blood spilled on every corner.

“I have been waiting for your return,” said the enormous figure. “I knew the day would come that you would show your face again.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Excuse our untimely intrusion, we do not come to cause any harm.”
</DeathVoice>

“I won’t fall on your excuses and trickery any more. My people have fallen because of it. If I must die here and now, then so be it.”

He stood up, taking his massive hammer. <span class="fx-impact">With a quick jump he struck the ground</span> where they were standing. Death was faster, she moved herself and her company to safety.

<DeathVoice>
  “I want you to take Ruppert and hide. Do not meddle on this, you could be seriously hurt,”
</DeathVoice>

she whispered to the girl.

“Are you ready to face me? Stop running from your fate. One of us will die tonight,” shouted the giant, about to strike again.

Death took her scissors out, diverting the attack gracefully. The warrior was open for a hit, but she didn’t hit back.

<DeathVoice>
  “Almir, great warrior. We mean no harm to you. We have no relation with your attackers, we just need to continue our journey.”
</DeathVoice>

He didn’t stop to hear. Taking some debris of the previous fights, he threw it on her way. Elana was standing behind her, she had to do something with the incoming rocks. A swing, multiple strikes. Everything got pulverized, shining with the gold of the scissors. Almir did not give her time to think. He struck again and again, without stop. With every hit, it became harder for her to block. In the middle of the chaos, Elana could find a place to avoid getting hurt. Behind some corpses, she took a shield and hid there. Odeia noticed that the girl was safe and proceeded to fight back. The giant didn't fight like a giant would. He had no grace in his attacks, he had no soul in his eyes. He was hollow, only a shell of his former self.

In maddening solitude, he had been awaiting to avenge his people. He had forgotten everything on his rage, waiting, hating, rotting. Only his will had survived, a will to kill. He continued to strike while Death cut him. <span class="fx-slash">She cut his tendons first,</span> making his weapon useless. He couldn’t move his arms. Then she went for the legs. As big as he was, this made them an easier target than anything. He fell to the floor, standing on his knees.

<DeathVoice>
  “Once again I tell you that we don’t want to cause more harm. It is very clear to us that you have had enough. Please, come to your senses and talk.”
</DeathVoice>

“There is nothing to say. Kill me already, like you did with my wife, my son, my people. The world is dying slowly, you can hear its heart pumping slower each day. I have nothing left, only my life. So take it away.”

<DeathVoice>
  “I am Death, daughter of the Sun. I don’t come to kill you. We just need to cross this desert.”
</DeathVoice>

The look on his eyes changed. He started to remember.

“If you are allies with the Sun, then I hate you even more. You are the reason why we hid underground.”

Odeia was astonished to hear that. She had never heard of the Sun hurting anyone.

<DeathVoice>
  “Why do you say that? Why would the Sun have business with the giants?”
</DeathVoice>

“You well know why. He hated us for we did not succumb to his tyranny. He despised us as much as he despised our parents. He made our lands a desert and struck us with his lightning. If it weren’t for our father Atorus, we would have perished long ago.”

<DeathVoice>
  “He attacked you? Have you been hiding from him all this time?”
</DeathVoice>

“Until he found our whereabouts and told the greedy men where we lived. Of course they came for us. The so-called church of salvation was responsible for all this death. They must pay, as well as everyone who serves the Sun.”

<DeathVoice>
  “This must be a mistake, my father would never do something of the sort.”
</DeathVoice>

“You just need to have a look around you. If your eyes can still deny the truth, then I cannot convince you otherwise.”

Odeia watched her surroundings. All the corpses in piles, all of them sharing the same banner.

“The Sun, the kingdom of Oldraan, the church of salvation. They are the ones responsible for this, and if you are his daughter, then you are just as responsible. What horrible deeds did you make for him?”

<DeathVoice>
  “I was not aware of what you tell me—”
</DeathVoice>

“Yet you kept fulfilling his doing without asking why. <span class="fx-key">Your ignorance does not excuse your faults.</span> I despise your entire existence. Now, if you excuse me, I’m getting disgusted by your mere presence. Kill me, if you must. Otherwise, leave.”

<DeathVoice>
  “We’ll leave now.”
</DeathVoice>

“GO. And never come back. You’ll wish for my death. If I ever see you again, my hammer won’t be so kind.”

Behind the throne was a lift. Elana got in with Ruppert. Death pulled the lever. It <span data-action="rise">took them up slowly</span>. Behind them they left Almir, screaming without stop. He was already dead, but his will would not want to stop fighting. The lift ascended, on their way they could appreciate all the damage that the Sun and his followers had caused. Elana did not know what the giant had said, that's why she didn’t know how much it would affect Odeia what she said:

<span class="fx-key">“How twisted were the ones who partook on something like this. How horrible must it be to take a life.”</span>

Elana was oblivious to her words, she would notice it until much later, but they echoed deeply on Death. She took a step forward, she looked at herself on the golden scissors. She broke the lace on her neck, throwing it all down to the magma floor.

“What are you doing?” shouted Elana. “That is your weapon.”

<DeathVoice>
  “I won’t need it anymore.”
</DeathVoice>

The scissors and the bell <span class="fx-whisper">submerged and burned.</span> <span class="fx-key">She didn’t want to perpetuate the Sun’s doing any longer; she would not take any more lives.</span> The only thing she had in the world was a cat and a girl. And her promise to protect them.
