<div data-scene="I. THE CANYON" aria-hidden="true"></div>

Long after hope had abandoned the heart of men and the blades they wielded served no purpose anymore; when oceans were dry and mountains laid flat and the trees were as hard as stone; when the days were dark and cold as night and nothing could grow on the lands; when the gods had been gone and their essence was lost, and their magic was buried in the mud; when the mere taste of food became no more than a wish and the sweet smell of flowers was nothing but a dream; when all those things had long been gone, <span class="fx-key fx-scatter">Death surprised herself being bored.</span>

<p class="fx-word-drift">She had forgotten how long it had been since her services were last needed, and she counted the days on her hands, <em>but her fingers weren't enough.</em> This was strange for her; she had never gotten that much free time before. Sitting at the edge of a canyon, her eyes tried to look at the horizon, but futile was her attempt—for sky and land <span class="fx-stretch">merged together in the distance.</span></p>

Death loomed over the edge, dangling her feet over the cliff. She was tall, about four meters of height. Her figure looked slender for the size she had; after all, she was **only a cluster of aged bones.** Her gaze twinkled while looking away. She had no physical eyes, yet a faint spark shined in her eye sockets. Around her neck she carried a bell, as gold and shiny as the crown she had over her head. The crown—*a useless symbol that she conserved*—was only a foolish reminder of her state as a goddess. A black cloth wrapped her, covering most of her body except for her white hair and skull-like face.

Her sight usually frightened kids and men alike. Tales were told to keep children behaving properly, and even soldiers feared she would appear on their night watches. She was, however, extremely peaceful, never causing direct harm to anyone.

<span class="fx-whisper fx-scatter">Except for that one time.</span>

She remembered the war, the only time she had fought without provocation. The bell on her neck—which told her about the souls to be taken—had guided her to participate. She had helped her father, *the Sun*, to end his enemies. It was strange that the bell did that; she only took the life of old and sick people. After the war had ended, it went back to its normal function. It was the first time that her bell had rung in a war, and it was also the last. When men killed each other, she didn’t act—that was not part of her duty. Sadly, her father had perished fighting against humans. <span class="fx-highlight">Now the world had sunk in darkness.</span>

  <div class="book-marginalia__inner">NOTE 01 // THE BLEEDING SUN — ECLIPSED OVER ATORUS</div>
</aside>

<p data-ambient="bleeding-sun">The Sun shined, for lack of better words, in front of her. Its light was of a <strong>dim red</strong>, and from it poured a constant stream of light that fell to the world. <em>It usually leaked at this hour.</em></p>

<Silence height="sm" ornament="dot" />

<p class="fx-word-drift" data-drift="ash">The canyon had a small town at the bottom where now only rubble and ruins remained. The few buildings still standing were covered by layers of dirt that built up over the years, making most of them inaccessible. The old church, broken to the core, had its bell shattered on the floor, and from the ruins raised columns of smoke that went to the sky, where they merged in a mass of grays. This landscape was not uncommon at all, as there was nothing but shambles of what was left of the world. Empty fields, abandoned kingdoms, dread and despair; everything covered by ashes, fire, or mud.</p>

<p class="fx-word-drift">Death had been sitting there for a while, knowing not how much. She didn’t have a concept of time as others would, so she tried to ground her perception of it. For a being who had such a long existence, moments started to <span class="fx-stretch">blend together.</span> Minutes felt like hours when she didn’t have something to do, and in the blink of a second a week could have been gone.</p>

In the past she was constantly occupied fulfilling her oath—famine and sickness had kept her busy—so she never had a moment to reflect. Over the thousands of years she had been in service of the Sun, she had never found herself bored before. In essence her job was simple: take the sorrows of life away from the dying souls, **cutting the divine link to the mother Moon** when their time arrived. But the time never came anymore, and she could only wonder why.

She looked at the tool she used to cut the thread of life—<span class="fx-sharp">a pair of large golden scissors</span>—and saw herself looking back in the reflection. She saw her own image, but didn't recognize herself. It was her face, no doubt of that, yet it seemed distorted, tainted by the golden reflection. *She wondered if that was the face of the last being in the world.*

Death knew that the gods had left a long time ago, though she didn’t know why. She had only seen the fight against the Sun firsthand, **a war to end all things.** All of her companions had abandoned her; even the stars were gone. Only the Sun had kept its place—some of it, at least—his body hung where he once shined. Its center was gone, resembling a constant eclipse whose faint light still embraced the dying Atorus, the world.

<DeathVoice>
  <span class="fx-scatter">“Am I alone?”</span>
</DeathVoice>

She asked herself loudly, and those were the first words she ever pronounced. It wasn’t that she couldn’t speak before, only that she didn’t see the use for it. She did what she had without asking explanations and without giving one. She tried to fill the <span class="fx-stretch">silence,</span> as it was uncomfortable to her. Her voice was tender, like the one of a woman, and it echoed in the distance.

<DeathVoice>
  “If I were to be, what use is there for me? With no souls to cut, no one left, <span class="fx-key fx-scatter" data-scatter-dir="fall">I’m no more than the ashes falling from the skies.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

All these feelings were unusual to Death; in fact, she had never felt anything at all. All this time she had been a force of duty, and now the lack of it resulted in **a discomforting emptiness,** and that emptiness was all she had left. Confused, she waited on that canyon for a sound that never came. She stood up and walked away from that desolated place with its smoky ruins. She began wandering again.

<Silence height="sm" ornament="dot" />

<div data-scene="II. THE BELL" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<aside class="book-marginalia book-marginalia--right" aria-hidden="true">

<p class="fx-word-drift">Death found an old road covered by mud not too far from where she stood. Her toes felt the wetness of the soil as she started walking up the path. Her walk looked tired; even though her movements were very agile, she still had an aura of an old woman. Old indeed she was, as old as life itself—you could tell it by the color of her bones. Her body was crooked in ways a physical body would be unable to bend. A tall black figure moving around. <em>A ghost of the past.</em></p>

She used the pair of scissors to help her walk; they were large enough for that—longer than a tall man. A constant <span class="fx-impact">clank</span> of the scissors hitting the rubble marked her arrival, followed by the cloth that covered her being dragged through the floor. On her belt hung a small bag, dangling as she stepped. It was the only thing she carried. Her entire appearance told a tale of aging and decaying; after all, she was a live representation of this barren world. <span class="fx-highlight">The only one remaining.</span>

<span class="fx-whisper fx-scatter">Or so she thought.</span>

<p class="fx-word-wave">Suddenly, the sound of her footsteps was interrupted by the bell on her neck, but this was no normal bell. For the ears of any sane person, it would be <span class="fx-chime">the most beautiful sound that could be heard.</span> Of course, no one was there to hear it. Death knew well what this sound meant and smiled to her own surprise. She had never been happy about someone dying before, but the endless wait for that sweet sound made it even better. <em>The time had come for someone.</em></p>

<MoodShift theme="dusk" label="THE BELL SINGS" />

She quickly started looking for the nearest building. Soon enough, she found a crumbling house with most of its walls torn apart, but a corner was left almost intact. She put her hands in the middle of both walls, right where the angle met, and <span class="fx-cleave">started to pull them apart</span>. Instead of the wall getting destroyed, a narrow hole came to be. Almost like tearing reality apart, <span class="fx-scatter">a vortex opened between two corners of existence.</span> Bigger was the hole now, big enough for her to fit in, and so she did. She peeked in the hole—<span class="fx-ghost">and then she wasn't anymore.</span>

<p class="fx-word-wave">On the inside of a modest building, her figure materialized. She knew the soul was close for the sound of it. The bell started to produce a sweet sound, not a jingle this time, more like <span class="fx-chime">a singing voice.</span> Death started to hum with it.</p>

<span class="fx-highlight">She exited the building through a window</span>, because the door was trapped by debris. As she exited, the sky—that was almost always gray—looked somehow happier. As if her mood was directly altering the world, she smiled and continued where the song took her.

<div data-scene="III. THE TOWN" aria-hidden="true"></div>

<h2 data-eyebrow="PART II // THE SONG">The town</h2>

She walked through the empty streets of a small town until she arrived at what looked to be an old plaza. The song became brighter—*that was the place*—but she could not perceive a single soul nearby. She <span class="fx-impact">nailed her scissors to the ground.</span> Confused, she started to heave the rocks of the collapsed buildings, as the bell could not be wrong. She went where the bell dragged her. **She found it.**

<div data-scene="IV. THE FLOWER" aria-hidden="true"></div>

It then became apparent what it was: <span class="fx-key fx-scatter">a blue flower squished by a rock. Most probably the last flower.</span> A beautiful sight to witness. Death stopped smiling right then. She thought there were no flowers left, because how could something as beautiful as this live amongst a world that had nothing but Death? She remembered another time, that now felt like another world. *A blue garden full of the same type of flowers, and Death in the midst of it, resting before taking away her next soul.* Smells and sounds came to her mind. Memories of a past she believed forgotten.

She laid down beside that delicate flower in the same way she used to do.

<DeathVoice>
  “I've done this since the beginning of time, but now my duty—”
</DeathVoice>

She sighed and stopped. She couldn’t finish her thought.

Death looked at the flower as if she were waiting for a response. She took a deep breath, mostly symbolic as she had no need to do so. She sat at its side and put her hands on the soil and squished it hard. The dirt poured from her fingers, squishy and wet. Death didn't know what this feeling was; she only knew emotions by mere observation and had never experienced something like this for herself. **The bell stopped singing** and the sound changed—now it resembled the bell of a church, <span class="fx-stretch">deep and resonant.</span>

<DeathVoice>
  <span class="fx-ritual">“Your time among us, as it's only natural, has come to an end. I thereby must take your life away. Your death brings me no sadness nor joy. I can only hope you can find your rest. Your absence will be mourned by those you leave behind. Sleep well, my friend. This is only another night.”</span>
</DeathVoice>

The words that came from her mouth were dry and bitter.

<p class="fx-word-wave">She touched the thread hanging from the flower towards the sky, and from it emerged <span class="fx-chime">a beautiful sound.</span> In the past, all living beings had a thread coming out of them, only visible to those who knew how to look. Death missed, in a way, the concert of strings and bells that a dying soul produced in their ultimate moments—a harmony formed throughout an entire life, just to be heard once at the end of it. And this particular sound was captivating; she stopped for a second to embody this melody in her memory. <em>This could well be the last time she heard it.</em></p>

Flowers were precious to her; she saw that their fragility made them special. Something about their short span of life gave them a unique place in the world. <span class="fx-key">Their existence having an end made them beautiful</span>—she thought the same about other pure forms of life.

<span class="fx-highlight">But everything must die,</span> she thought. She could make no exceptions, no matter how much it hurt her.

On her knees, Death gave the flower **a last kiss.**

<DeathVoice>
  <span class="fx-scatter">“This is but another dreamless night.</span> <span class="fx-whisper">Good night.”</span>
</DeathVoice>

<span class="fx-slash" data-thread="true">Snip.</span>

<MoodShift theme="ash" label="THE WORLD GROWS QUIETER" />

<p class="fx-word-drift" data-drift="ash">A dead flower laid on the ground as the bell rang no more. A single tear formed in her sockets, but it didn’t fall. The sky was sad, the world was <span class="fx-stretch">a little quieter.</span></p>

She held the withered plant on her palms and knew what she wanted. <span class="fx-key">To find beauty, something worth living for.</span> Now she thrived for a thing that may not exist anymore, but she had until the end of days to find it.

<span class="fx-whisper fx-scatter">And Death continued to wander.</span>
