Elana, with her shovel, lifted a chunk of the ground—the muddy soils had plenty of water. After filtering it, she prepared it to boil.

*I need to be fast*, she told herself.

Once the pot had reached boiling temperatures, she took it off the fire. She burned her hands holding the hot pot, but she didn’t care, she only wanted to see her father healthy again. She ran back to the building.

“Father,” she screamed, “the door is locked. Open it now; I have your water here.”

But no response came from the other side, only the silence greeted her. She tried to force herself into the room, but the door didn't budge; it was locked.

*What is happening?* Elana thought.

“Father, open right now. I have the water. OPEN.” She shouted and knocked, but no answer came back. After a few more tries, she went back to the cart.

Sword in hands, she returned and knocked once more. After a silent second, she <span class="fx-cleave">started to chop the door</span>. Many swings did it take, but eventually the door had an opening big enough for her to enter. Throwing the sword to the floor, she entered the room pushing a chair that blocked it.

<span class="fx-whisper">Oh—.</span>

Her pupils dilated, her jaw dropped. She held her arms to her face trying to cover the sight—as if that would make it less real.

There she met her father—still warm to the touch—with his body laying down. She didn’t need to get close, she already knew. His hand hung from one side of the bed. He had a relaxed expression but his lungs breathed no more.

<span class="fx-whisper">She had no words.</span>

She shook him to wake him up, but <span class="fx-ghost">the life of his eyes had gone away.</span> Taking some of the water she brought, she opened his mouth and forced it down. No response. The father was dead.

After doing everything she could, she finally desisted. Knees on floor, she broke down in tears. She buried her face on the sheets, gripping his hand with strength. No words could describe the suffering she went through in that moment. <span class="fx-key">She was alone. Alone for the first time in her life.</span> So, she wept and cried until her voice abandoned her and her eyes were dry.

An hour passed. Once she calmed, and finally looked up at her father, she squeezed the old man. A long cold hug, not for him, but for her.

“Father, my dear father. What happened to you? One second you are as strong as a bear and the next—” she trailed off.

*This doesn’t make sense.*

She was left with no words to add, she stayed there speechless.

“I know what you need,” she said. “You are just a bit cold. I’ll bring you some fire.”

She went back to the cart, where she made the fire before. She took some lit sticks and brought them with her. On the floor of the room, she set some rocks and tried to make a quick flame. With the sheets that his father used, she made a big fire.

“Now, father. You can get warm, you just need some rest.”

The fire grew bigger, the room got warmer.

“Can you feel it? Can you feel the fire?”

No answer, of course. The fire began to expand, the wooden floor gave it an easy way to do so. Growing bigger and hotter, soon it reached the bed. Elana jumped. She took the pot full of water and threw it to the fire. It did nothing. She didn’t know what to do. In a hurry, she took the body on her hands. It was heavy and stiff so she dragged it through the floor.

Smoke filled the room, black and dense. She couldn’t see but remembered the layout of the building. The wooden house was quick to burn, the fire already reached the ceiling and began on the other story. Elana got to the stairs; she would need to drag her father over them. She coughed.

*Stupid*, she thought.

The thick smoke didn’t allow her to breathe, she wanted to throw up. Her head felt light and the small light coming from upstairs was blurry.

*I can’t do this. I’m going to die with him.*

She looked at the old man on the floor, his face barely resembled her father.

*He’s gone*, she thought. <span class="fx-key">*There is nothing to save.*</span>

She decided to go upstairs, but something caught her attention. The locket was on the floor, it got stuck on her father’s leg. She took it, running away. On her way, she also picked up the sword that she had thrown away. With struggle, she found her way out. She exited the building amidst smoke and fire.

<MoodShift theme="void" label="RAIN &amp; THUNDER" />

On her back, the whole building was tumbling. She could still feel the fire on her skin and smoke on her lungs; completely covered by soot. She coughed. Her eyes hurt, her head was spinning, but she made it out.

A drop of water on the floor, then another, then even more. <span class="fx-key">Rain and thunder broke the skies, falling like the tears on her cheeks.</span> She wiped her face with the water, then she screamed. A howl of pain, her screams fused with the noise of the building <span class="fx-impact">collapsing</span>. It fell. Not even rain could stop the fires.

It kept burning until nothing was left to burn. <span class="fx-stretch">The rain continued all night.</span>

Elana stood there the entire time. With the locket on her hand and the sword on another, she could only watch. The fires extinguished and the weather improved, but her heart was still in pain. She buried her face on her knees.

<Silence height="sm" ornament="dot" />

“I know what it is that I must do,” she said. <span class="fx-key">“I will continue with our journey for the both of us.</span> I’ll take you to the place you longed to be. I’ll reach the city of gods.”

She took the locket and put it on her neck. She lifted the sword—wrapped in a cloth—and secured it on her belt. Stepping away, she looked at the things left on the floor, they didn’t hold any value to her. Everything that she needed was on her bag. After she took the map and compass, she made a big fire. The cart, the things, even supplies. Everything went to the flames.

<span class="fx-key">“May you dance with the moon until the end of days,”</span> Elana said as she walked away only holding her few belongings. The north awaited her, and she had to go alone.
