The lift took them to the surface. The sky was brighter than before; it was morning already.

The sand swallowed the elevator, leaving no trail of it behind. They were in the middle of the desert and still had a long way to travel. Elana had a look of the map, took a sip of water and walked towards the Sun. Ruppert and Odeia followed behind.

“Did we just witness the last giant die?” Elana asked as she moved. The things they saw were hard to process, everything happened too fast.

<DeathVoice>
  “We did not only witness it. I was the one who killed him. The one to end his entire lineage.”
</DeathVoice>

She didn’t have facial expressions, it would be impossible, but her face showed signs of preoccupation.

“You sound concerned by it. Haven’t you done this thousands of times?”

She stopped walking and looked at Elana.

<DeathVoice>
  “Done what? Take a life? That’s the problem; I’m the problem.”
</DeathVoice>

“You tried to reason with him, but he failed to listen. He lost his mind on hatred. He was already dead when we arrived, you saw it. Just a dead body with its will still fighting.”

<DeathVoice>
  “I was the one who didn’t listen. He was screaming in pain and I did not listen. We could have solved things in a different manner. This is the first time… that I fight for myself, I had never taken a life that wasn’t mine to take. This was different.”
</DeathVoice>

“What about your hand? You have fought before. What is different now?”

Odeia took her amputated limb out.

<DeathVoice>
  “Sometimes I would encounter souls who are too stubborn to go.”
</DeathVoice>

She moved her arm closer to the girl so that she could see it well.

<DeathVoice>
  “This is how they would fight to keep on living. Almir, on the other hand, did not hear the sound of the bells, it wasn’t his time. That is the big difference.”
</DeathVoice>

“It was either him or us. Either way, none would’ve stopped until the other was dead. You were just protecting us. I thought your word was your law.”

<DeathVoice>
  “He could have done harm to you; you’re right on that. I cannot permit something of the sort to occur. You speak truth, I’m being foolish. Forget my words.”
</DeathVoice>

She resumed her pace and took the lead.

<DeathVoice>
  “Soon, we’ll reach the end of these lands. We need to hasten our pace, otherwise we’ll spend another night here.”
</DeathVoice>

“Agreed,” answered the girl and walked faster to match her speed. The more they advanced the easier it got to move on the desert. It didn’t take them long before they started to see shapes on the horizon, the end of the desert was near. They walked until the soil changed its texture. Not mud, nor sand, but firm land. Crooked trees grew closer to them; they were entering another forest. Their knees felt tired, their feet swollen. After half a day of travel, Ruppert decided to take a nap on a fallen tree. At the sight of him peacefully resting, they agreed to stop. After all, Elana hadn’t slept since the day before. Soon the cat was cuddling with her. Odeia remaining in silence, burned her thoughts in the fire.

*Could the Sun be responsible for the genocide of giants?* she asked herself time and time again. She was fiddling with the burning coal. *Why would he hate them? Why would he bring death to them? Why would he bring me?* She doubted her father and herself. Something so strange for her that her empty stomach twisted. A bad feeling in the gut she had felt since she talked with Almir.

<DeathVoice>
  “I will not continue with your duty. <span class="fx-key">I will not take another life until I have the answers.</span> I cannot keep following your desire blindly.”
</DeathVoice>

She was talking to the flames. The Sun was high up in the sky, the trail of blood it left fell down to the world, lighting the trees around them with its red light. She now wanted to reach the city of gods as much as Elana did.

<Silence height="sm" ornament="dot" />

<MoodShift theme="ash" label="THE FOG OF MISSING SOULS" />

They resumed with their journey. The zone they were in was filled with trees scattered sparsely, dense enough to not see the horizon. As they walked, their vision became impaired; they were <span class="fx-ghost">covered by fog.</span> Elana struggled to continue, she was out of breath.

*This doesn’t feel right*, Odeia thought. She stopped and looked around. The trees went up to the sky, getting lost in the distance. Everything looked as usual, calm. <span class="fx-stretch">Too calm to be expressed by words,</span> dead. She felt it in her bones, an eerie feeling through her spine.

<DeathVoice>
  “<span class="fx-whisper">Try to not breathe,</span>”
</DeathVoice>

she said addressing the girl. She took Ruppert on her arms and covered him on her cloak.

<DeathVoice>
  “There’s something wrong in this mist.”
</DeathVoice>

Elana felt the goosebumps in her arms, she started to sweat. A cold sweat. The powerful god of death was seriously preoccupied by something in that forest. She just saw her beat the king of giants without much effort, what could possibly scare a god?

<span class="fx-sharp">A scream came from the woods.</span>

“You said there was nothing left alive other than us,” the girl said trembling.

<DeathVoice>
  “That’s what I fear. What is there creeping around? It most certainly is not alive.”
</DeathVoice>

More screams, laments of despair. They didn’t come from just one source, rather they surrounded them. The fog was so dense that they had to walk with their arms extended. They kept moving, Death on the lead, extremely attentive. Odeia didn’t have her scissors, but that would have been a great moment to use them. Elana wielded her old sword, still wrapped by the cloth, she was shaking.

<DeathVoice>
  “You don’t have to worry,”
</DeathVoice>

said Death. She started sniffing around. Like a hound, she got on her six extremities and crawled following a smell.

<DeathVoice>
  “I know what stinks.”
</DeathVoice>

“What is it?” She had not stopped trembling. Her grip was weak, her stance frail.

<DeathVoice>
  “That is the smell of dead. We’re surrounded by it.”
</DeathVoice>

“What are you talking about?”

<DeathVoice>
  “The souls that are yet to leave this world. The souls who died before it was due. Quick, get on my back. We need to get some air.”
</DeathVoice>

Elana jumped on her back, held by the lower set of arms. She handed the cat to the girl and climbed one of the stronger trees. The screams continued, they were suffering. The fog disappeared after a few meters in the air. The girl could finally breathe without difficulties. Her head was dizzy and she felt numb.

They looked down. White figures walked with the fog, they were only clumps of it. They dragged themselves slowly, some fell, some crawled, but they all seemed to be going in the same direction. North. The red Sun. They walked together not acknowledging each other, they moved like they weren’t alive. They weren’t. They were only souls without a shell.

“Ghosts,” Elana said.

<DeathVoice>
  “I do not like the term, but indeed. Those are what your species call ghosts.”
</DeathVoice>

“Why was the air so dense down there?”

<DeathVoice>
  “They are not meant to be on this world, their essence is vanishing. <span class="fx-key">You were breathing the remains of souls, they evaporate.</span> They died with a strong will to live, now strolling the world, <span class="fx-ghost">having forgotten their own names.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

“Are they dangerous?” Elana asked.

<DeathVoice>
  “Possibly. Most of them aren’t. Some only had difficult lives, others had horrible deaths. It depends on the last intent they had, their unfinished business.”
</DeathVoice>

“Can we get down now? I don’t think they will hurt us, they are only passing by.”

The figures didn’t stop at anything, they walked as if they didn’t even notice each other. Moving as a big group, almost in a formation sort of way, marching toward their enemy. They had swords and other weapons. They were warriors.

“They look like an army,” Elana added. “They are going north, where the Sun is, the same as us. Maybe they had unfinished business with the Sun.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Everyone seems to do. We cannot go down. You can’t breathe in the fog.”
</DeathVoice>

“It is a bit difficult, but not impossible. I can walk normally.”

<DeathVoice>
  “No, you don’t understand. I mean that you CAN’T. They are the fog itself. <span class="fx-key">Their grief is contagious, one could lose his mind if he spends too much time in the mists.</span> You would end up sharing their way and finish like one of them. But that is not what I fear.”
</DeathVoice>

She stopped talking, not wanting to share what really bothered her.

“What is it?” Elana asked. “What else is there in the mists?”

<DeathVoice>
  “Something far more dangerous than simple missing souls. Something wicked. A mistcrawler.”
</DeathVoice>

“Mistcrawler?” She had never heard of those before.

<DeathVoice>
  “It is normal that you wouldn't know about it. The mists can be dangerous enough by themselves. Finding a mistcrawler... Let’s just say that not many humans have survived being near one, let alone the sight of one.”
</DeathVoice>

<MoodShift theme="void" label="A MISTCRAWLER IN THE WOODS" />

Deep in the forest, a tumult could be heard. A cluster of heartbreaking screams. This wasn’t like the other laments of the night, this was far worse. Even at a distance, the sound made Elana cover her ears.

<DeathVoice>
  “And it seems like we’re near one. Let’s hope we haven’t got his attention. We’ll have to move through the woods. You better not fall.”
</DeathVoice>

The god <span data-action="surge">jumped from branch to branch</span>. She didn’t give much time to Elana to hold stronger. Her jumps were strong enough to pull out the roots, and throw the trunks away. If Odeia wasn’t holding the girl, she would have fallen. Ruppert only slept as if nothing was happening, already used to the harsh movement. The noise of the wood hitting the floor only soothed the screams below them. She left a trail of fallen trees behind, going down the valley.

*If she doesn’t want to bring attention to us, then she sucks at it*, Elana thought. She wanted to scream but didn’t want to attract anything on the mists. They descended more and more until they reached the bottom of the Valley. They passed a few small buildings on their way.

Deep Valley was a picturesque place on its better days. Before the third conquest, it marked the reach of The Empire, just before the land of giants. The architecture looked a lot more alike the normal cities closer to the Capitol. They had made sure to build over the old structures that there stood. Erasing any trace of previous cultures. Its citizens didn’t share much similarities to the more conventional Oldraan people, after all they were annexed after the battle of Deilos, the second conquest. They kept a lot of the facial features the skaa, the original inhabitants of these lands had.

“We should stay in one of the buildings. Wait until the fog is gone,” Elana said.

<DeathVoice>
  “That would be a good idea. Sadly it is not that easy. If it is true that we share our destination with the warriors, then we are sure to meet our paths again. We have to move faster than them.”
</DeathVoice>

Elana had not thought of that.

<DeathVoice>
  “Besides, a mistcrawler could smell our scent. Just as well as I can smell the dead, they can smell the alive. No building is capable of containing them. I’ve seen castles fall after a dense mist.”
</DeathVoice>

The more she knew, the more the girl grew curious about what was hiding in there. What is it like? What could possibly produce such a terrible sound? Elana was uncertain, extremely frightened, but still curious.

<Silence height="sm" ornament="dot" />

<MoodShift theme="ash" label="OUTRUNNING THE WHITE BLANKET" />

In the top of a tall tree, Death stopped to have a look around. She had left the ocean of souls behind, but it wasn’t far away, still advancing towards them. *I think I lost them*, she thought. The white blanket was broad, covering her entire field of view. If it was an army, then it must be the army of the Empire of Sun. Only they could maintain such large number. *Ironic*, she thought and kept on moving.

Elana and Ruppert were tired, they felt heavy on her back. She knew that traveling at such speed was exhausting for creatures as delicate as them. She decided to let them rest.

An hour passed since they exited the fog and she found a clearing in the woods for them to sleep. With the distance she gained, they could sleep a full night and continue at Sunrise. She saw the girl too tired to set camp so with a swing of an arm, <span class="fx-impact">multiple trees fell.</span> She made a fire big enough to heat a house and, tearing some trunks in half, she made an improvised bed.

<DeathVoice>
  “It’s rough, but I hope it is of your pleasing.”
</DeathVoice>

“I’m well accustomed to sleep on the hard rocks,” Elana said. “But thank you, it is a great gesture of you.” She let her body fall on the wood. The cat found his way on her chest, making her uncomfortable to move but warm at the same time. “I never commented about it before—with the mists and all that—but it’s weird to find mountains and valleys still standing.”

<DeathVoice>
  “What about them?”
</DeathVoice>

“I’ve traveled half the world for about half my life. I’ve never come to see a mountain, not even once. Everywhere I went to was always as flat as dead. What happened to them?”

<DeathVoice>
  “I see. I guess they were fairly common in the earlier days.”
</DeathVoice>

Elana frowned waiting on her to expand on her answer. The goddess looked at her and turned to the skies as if she would find an answer in them. It was cloudy and dark.

<DeathVoice>
  “You look at me with intrigue in your eyes. Sadly, I know as much as you do about these matters. I’m unaware of the state of the world, but if I had to guess… Then I would tell you that most probably Atorus is dead. His land is just feeling his absence. I could tell you about the life before the unlit days. However, I’m oblivious at anything leading to the eternal Sunset.”
</DeathVoice>

“The mountains. How were they?” Elana asked.

<DeathVoice>
  “In a word, magnificent.”
</DeathVoice>

Odeia had a shine on her look.

<DeathVoice>
  “An imposing sight to witness, to be sure. The taller ones were full of snow and the lower ones—full of color and flowers.”
</DeathVoice>

“Flowers? Like the one you gave me?”

<DeathVoice>
  “Yes. Like the one I gave you.”
</DeathVoice>

Odeia smiled.

“It’s hard to imagine such things existed. With the world we live in—” A howl in the distance interrupted her thought. She seemed worried about the mist on their back and tried to watch it in the horizon. They were too far to see it, but the sound still reached them.

*They aren’t far*, Odeia thought. *We can’t afford to lose much time.*

“What is exactly a mistcrawler?” Elana asked. Her eyes twinkling with the flames. “Why do they exist?”

<DeathVoice>
  “It is hatred, mourn and despair. I’ve found that the strongest of wills leave the greater traces. Mistcrawlers are evil, they were evil in life and they only rotten even more in death.”
</DeathVoice>

“But why do they sound so—”

<DeathVoice>
  “Ungodly? They aren’t only a soul, rather than a mass of them. <span class="fx-key">Souls that came together in the same being, that is why they sound to have many voices.</span> In a way, they weren’t even created, they came to existence by their own will. They are some of the most dangerous beings in Atorus.”
</DeathVoice>

"That’s horrible,” Elana said.

<DeathVoice>
  “Indeed it is. It is the result of many unprepared souls. They died before their time, I never lifted them from their worldly pain. Such a sorrowful incident.”
</DeathVoice>

“Then, the world without you would be filled with them?” Elana said with her eyes almost closed.

The goddess had never contemplated that possibility. It was frightening to imagine that many souls loose. *The girl is right*, she thought. *Maybe my responsibilities did play a role for the better.*

Elana was falling asleep. Her eyes were heavy and mid-talk she started to snore. Odeia lay down on the trunk, looking above her. She remembered older times and how she once filled the sky with stars. <span class="fx-key">Death was also important, she thought. Death also had a purpose.</span> Maybe I’m not only the whim of the Sun. Almir could be wrong. <span class="fx-whisper">Maybe…</span>

She rested easy for the rest of the night.
