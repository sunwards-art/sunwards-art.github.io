The mist of dust settled on the ground. Death had been sitting in the plaza, in front of the Cathedral, looking at what she did.

<DeathVoice>
  “These feelings of mine aren’t proper for a god. Anger, such a useless emotion. The mere thought of me losing my nerve for the past actions of a few humans is inconceivable.”
</DeathVoice>

But she couldn’t help herself. If she had had any veins, they would’ve been about to explode. Her four fists tightly clenched. Her scissors on the ground.

<DeathVoice>
  “Even so, I had already come and taken the life of the people in here. How could I obnoxiously ignore the evil that was being perpetrated?”
</DeathVoice>

She paused to look at her scissors.

<DeathVoice>
  “No, even if I had known, I wouldn’t have moved a finger. How foolish of me.”
</DeathVoice>

Death stood up and shook off the dirt. With her scissors as a cane, she walked away. As she moved, her feet left a mark on the ground but it was quickly covered by the falling particles. Kicking the flasks and stepping on the corpses, she continued without looking back. At the distance, the city looked exactly the same as she found it, and Death’s journey went on.

<Silence height="sm" ornament="dot" />

Elana woke up just to see Ruppert over her head. For some reason he preferred the heat of her forehead, even more so than the one the fire provided.

*Maybe he feels safe this way*, she thought. It was to her liking, definitely, even when she had to endure neck pain for a few hours each morning. She didn’t mind at all, she had come to appreciate it—expect it even. Each morning, as she stood up, he would rub against her legs—making a dance around her. They were quite fond of each other for such a short time being together.

<DeathVoice>
  “We must continue, my lady. There’s still a long way ahead of us and I’m afraid that—”
</DeathVoice>

She pointed at Deilos, the city they had left behind the day prior. The mist was coming in their way.

<DeathVoice>
  “It would benefit us to keep our advantage, they don’t need to stop at night. However, if we were to keep traveling while you sleep—”
</DeathVoice>

“You’ve done plenty enough already, I’m very grateful for that. You know that this is something I must do for myself.”

Odeia nodded.

The mists of souls were not fast, but it didn’t slow down at any obstacle. It certainly would become a problem if they stopped for long enough. They had to keep moving.

*You can only stay in a place for so long. It’s always bad if you grow attached to it. It could be days, it could be hours. Always keep moving*, she remembered the words of her father. Advice she thought wise to apply to people too. She still thought about her father constantly. The harsh emotions had soothed with the days, but the pain was still there. Like an open wound, she felt his absence on her chest. She didn’t think about the pain often, however, as she had always something else in mind. Something she had to thank for her new group of travelers.

*He would like the companions I’ve found along the way*, she thought, but felt weird to call them that. *Friends*, she corrected. She didn’t know them for long but they were her friends. They cared for her and she started to care for them as well.

She felt the voice of doubt in her head and she remembered the vial on her pocket.

She did not resent Odeia for taking the life of her father anymore. She knew it was his time to go. But there was a lingering feeling of distrust.

*He killed your father*, the voice said. She ignored the thought.

The terrain felt more inclined and harder to walk. They were over a hill that used to serve as a vineyard: the whole landscape was filled by them. No matter how vertical their path was, it was a joy for her to use the imperial roads. Her feet never felt so relieved to step over something other than mud or sand.

She paused and lost her thoughts looking at the horizon. She tried to imagine the colors of the hills but couldn’t do it. She had to take out her flower—the one Odeia gave her—and picture them with the same color. A deep and beautiful blue.

Was the sky the same shade of blue? Impossible to know for sure. She did, however, regain the wonder in the world.

*I wonder*, she thought.

“Are there any ways to bring back the dead?” Elana asked.

<DeathVoice>
  “None.”
</DeathVoice>

“I'm sure that you would know. You’re the goddess of death. There must be a way.”

Odeia frowned.

<DeathVoice>
  “Death is permanent. <span class="fx-key">There is nothing that can bring someone back.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

“What about the gods? You can live forever, you have the power.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Silly humans, always attributing all kind of abilities to us. No. We can’t do anything of the sort.”
</DeathVoice>

“But gods are immortals.”

<DeathVoice>
  “How well did that work out? Do you see many gods going around these days? If my beliefs are confirmed, then all gods are gone. If we had such powers, the unlit days wouldn’t have started, wouldn’t they?”
</DeathVoice>

Elana doubted for a second. “Then what about this?” She showed a small vial with some liquid inside. Cyan, shiny, the bottle had the same fluids that flowed in Deilos.

The goddess was notably angry.

<DeathVoice>
  “Where did you get that? You shouldn’t play with things you are yet to understand. Hand that to me.”
</DeathVoice>

Her tone was loud and demanding.

“No,” Elana replied. “We humans aren’t the fools you make us to be. I saw how they fought for this thing. I saw what it did.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Then you know how much damage it can cause. You may not fully understand it, but you saw it. An entire city died for that thing.”
</DeathVoice>

“Everyone died, you said it yourself. But this—” She was looking at the small vial, its contents were hypnotizing in a sense. “This can give life. I saw it. If I had this before—”

<DeathVoice>
  “Then your father would still be alive?”
</DeathVoice>

“Yes,” Elana said angrily.

<DeathVoice>
  “Do you think I’m lying to you? Do you truly believe that my desire is to harm you in any way?”
</DeathVoice>

“I don’t care what you desire. I only know that you took my father away and this would have saved him.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Yes, he would still be here if he ingested that vial.”
</DeathVoice>

Odeia tried to search for the better words.

<DeathVoice>
  “But it wouldn’t be him. His body would be alive but his soul would be bound to a world where it doesn’t belong.”
</DeathVoice>

“What are you trying to say?”

<DeathVoice>
  “Have you heard of the <span class="fx-chime">thread of life?</span>”
</DeathVoice>

She tried to get closer to the girl but she backed off at her attempt.

<DeathVoice>
  “It is the line that all living beings have and share. It connects them together, when it's cut you die.”
</DeathVoice>

“And you cut the one my father had.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Yes. It had to be cut. The thing is that this thread is very flexible. It moves with you all your life, but it can only grow so long. <span class="fx-key">When it reaches its limit, I have to cut it.</span> If I don’t, well… You saw what can happen.”
</DeathVoice>

“Mistcrawlers?”

<DeathVoice>
  “Mistcrawlers. However, there are ways to unnaturally extend the thread. That thing on your hand is one of them.”
</DeathVoice>

“Then it could have saved my father.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Only at a great cost. His thread would have twisted, becoming tangled with itself or other threads. That’s what happened in Deilos. They all got corrupted and killed each other. <span class="fx-key">There is no way to elongate life, none that doesn’t destroy the thread in the process.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

Elana <span class="fx-impact">threw the vial to the floor</span>. She broke in tears. Odeia came to her and hugged her. Ruppert did not comprehend what the fuss was all about but he also consoled the girl.

“It hurts so much.”

<DeathVoice>
  “<span class="fx-whisper">I know.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

“I just want him to come back.”

<DeathVoice>
  “It is normal to lose and hurt, I would believe.”
</DeathVoice>

“How would you know? How would you understand this pain I feel?”

<DeathVoice>
  “I have lost too. We all have. Death is just another step in life. We can’t avoid it.”
</DeathVoice>

“I'm sorry,” Elana said sobbing. “I shouldn't treat you like that. You have been nothing but kind to me.”

<DeathVoice>
  “No need to apologize, my dear. Now, if you will, clean your face and follow me. The day is clear enough, you’ll be able to see it.”
</DeathVoice>

<span class="fx-whisper">*See it?*</span> Elana thought.

<MoodShift theme="dusk" label="THE OTHER SIDE OF ATORUS" />

Odeia stepped aside and went up the hill, she waved at her to go. Elana wiped her face and started to walk. She stopped for a second, she turned and kicked the vial. It <span class="fx-impact">hit a rock and broke,</span> its liquid poured on the road.

<DeathVoice>
  “There is something I want you to see,”
</DeathVoice>

said Odeia as Elana walked up to her.

They were on a mountain, the height and inclination of it didn’t show what was behind it. They had been following the road for about four hours and they finally reached the tip.

A <span class="fx-stretch">gigantic land mass</span> <span data-action="rise">rose in the horizon</span>. It was gigantic, it couldn’t fit in her view, she had to turn her head around just to see how it got <span class="fx-stretch">lost from east to west.</span>

“What is it?” Elana asked.

<DeathVoice>
  “That, my dear, is the world.”
</DeathVoice>

“Another world?” she asked in awe.

<DeathVoice>
  “No, silly. That is still Atorus. <span class="fx-key">The other side of it, to be precise.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

“I don’t understand.” Her eyes were still wet and shined with the blue of the world. “How is that possible?”

<DeathVoice>
  “Don’t tell me that your father did not speak about the shape of the world.”
</DeathVoice>

“Its shape? Isn’t Atorus a disk?”

<DeathVoice>
  “A disk? Now, that is a funny image. How could you think that? It’s preposterous.”
</DeathVoice>

Odeia laughed in a burlesque manner.

<DeathVoice>
  “What would you do if you reach the end of this disk of yours? Fall from the world?”
</DeathVoice>

Elana flushed. “Well, yeah.”

Odeia laughed.

<DeathVoice>
  “I guess that your people really lost most of your knowledge during the dark days. A toroidal shape, they called it. Though I wasn’t very fond of that description. Don’t worry, I’ll help you understand.”
</DeathVoice>

Odeia walked outside the stone road and drew something in the mud. A circle with a very wide circumference.

<DeathVoice>
  “We’re here,”
</DeathVoice>

she said pointing at a zone near the hole in the figure. She then turned to the sky.

<DeathVoice>
  “When the other side of Atorus is above our heads, <span class="fx-key">we’ll have reached the city of gods.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

“Our world is hollow?”

<DeathVoice>
  “Yes, that’s where the Sun is. The Empire, the center of the world.”
</DeathVoice>

“I thought it was just a name.”

<DeathVoice>
  “An accurate one at that.”
</DeathVoice>

She chuckled.

<DeathVoice>
  “You weren’t so wrong, though. In a way our world is nothing but a fat disk with a hole in the middle.”
</DeathVoice>

“A weird image indeed.”

They continued walking until the night came. The light reflected from the other side still hit them and let them see at night. It was lucky for them to find such a clear sky to witness the beautiful landscape. They had a long way still, but the stone roads would take most of the burden.

Elana lay on the ground, holding her locket in her hand. *You would love to see this*, she thought. She turned to see her friends. *You would really like them.*
