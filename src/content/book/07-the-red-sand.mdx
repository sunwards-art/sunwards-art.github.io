Death followed the girl closely. She had given her word and she planned to live by it: on the third day she would appear in front of her and take her to the city of gods.

The rain had stopped after an hour. Elana continued towards north, scarcely looking back. She feared that her legs would stop and come back for themselves if she kept looking. She left the town behind her, outside of it were the empty fields where crops once grew. The vast land was burning and filled with what seemed to be the leftovers of a war.

War. A concept that Elana could not comprehend. Why did the early humans kill one another? She couldn’t know, she barely had an understanding of the past thanks to her dad.

Dead weeds full of thorns covered the field in a never-ending fire. Corpses everywhere, but rather than bodies, empty shells of the warriors they were. They had kept their fighting stances, facing each other. That was the only thing that made them look human as now they were covered by a thick and black crust. Their skin and clothes had burned. Their weapons and armor had survived, stained in a dry red and covered by oxide. In the distance they looked like they were disintegrating. Some falling, some breaking. The sound of what appeared to be endless fighters being consumed by fire was a bad omen for the lonely girl. She felt it in her bones.

She followed the path until the figures were too close that they prevented her advance. She stopped and cleaned her nose and wiped her face. The fires and burnt figures surrounded her. With sticks she made a fire, filtered water and sat to rest. With the heat coming out from the boiling water, she warmed her hands. From her bag she took out her small notebook and a piece of coal and started to draw. The book was filled with drawings of the places she had visited before, it also contained a lot of portraits of her father. She tried to draw to keep her mind occupied. She couldn’t. Once the carbon touched the paper, she broke it in half. She wasn’t controlling her grip and squeezed it with all she had. Pieces of coal fell everywhere, some of them digging into her hand. The paper was wet in tears. She crushed the notebook on her hands and tossed it away. Getting up quickly she kicked it in the mud and it flew far away.

She drew the sword and approached one of the dark figures. Her furious face was reflected on the blade. With a strong swipe, <span class="fx-slash">the shape was cut in two</span>, then three, then four, and she kept doing it until the pieces were too small to cut. She went to the next one. The figures were easy to cut, they just fell apart. Exploding like the rage she held, they fell with their weapons to the ground and <span class="fx-ghost">disappeared.</span> Everything buried by mud and burnt in the ground.

“How could you abandon me?” she screamed.

Elana went from one figure to the next.

“We were so close. Why did you have to die?”

She struck another figure, then the next, shouting to each one of them, waiting an answer from her father. Breaking them all, she didn't stop until the last one had fallen. Then she fell too, exhausted. Hours had passed in that field. Death observed everything from afar. Waiting until she had calmed down.

Once the girl was sleeping, Death came and took her to the place where her rage was let loose. She made a fire and let the small cat walk freely for the first time that day. Then she covered the girl with her cloak. Elana didn’t rest well. The grief troubled her even on her dreams. Death tried to calm her with an old song she remembered, a song of blue flowers and a beautiful woman. She sang and Elana slept. And the night passed quietly.

Elana woke up, face on the floor, and looked at the empty fields. She didn’t remember when she passed out, she didn’t remember making a fire. She glanced at her surroundings. Not even the shadows were left. She let a desperate scream out, a gasp of pain. On the horizon a distant figure was left standing, it was the farthest one and Elana would not let it be. She saw her hands, sore and bleeding, and tried to lift the sword again but she was not able to do it. Again, she grabbed the sword and again she failed. Her hands were burning, but not as much as her heart did. She kneeled to get closer, grabbing with her teeth. Then she dragged herself to where the last one of the shells stood. Her pace was slow and her legs were heavy. For a long time, she walked, but her feet gave out. Then she crawled through the ground, still wet from the rain of the day before. Nothing mattered to her. She wanted to avenge his father, she wanted to destroy the shadows, she wanted to only hurt outside.

When she was at the feet of that statue, she looked at it from below. It wasn’t like the others. This one didn’t have a weapon, and wasn’t in a fighting position. This one seemed like it was running. Was it running from her? Impossible, but that’s how it felt. With struggle she stood up. Then she could have a better view of what the figure was holding. It looked like a baby. A woman running with its child at the moment when the unlit days began.

<span class="fx-impact">The sword fell to the ground.</span> Jaw wide open. She allowed herself to crumble to the floor. She couldn’t move. She didn’t want to. She only desired to be swallowed by the mud, become one with it. <span class="fx-ghost">Only feel the steps.</span>

“Why did you abandon me?” she asked.

“Who is that? Show yourself to me.”

Elana was facing the ground. Death was standing behind her. She walked slowly until they faced each other. Her eyes could not believe what they were seeing, she petrified by fear. The slender and tall creature bent her neck showing her skull head and said:

<DeathVoice>
  “He’s not gone, you know? <span class="fx-key">You still carry him on your heart.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

“W... Wha... What kind of monster are you?” managed to mumble Elana.

<DeathVoice>
  “Monster? I’m not a monster. I’m what some of you call the God of Death. I was the one who took your father away.”
</DeathVoice>

“Then you really are a monster,” she shouted. “Why would you do that? Why would you take him from me? We were so close... why couldn’t you wait?”

<DeathVoice>
  “Time waits for nobody, not even for me. It had to be done.”
</DeathVoice>

“WHY? Why did it have to be done? You have brought so much misery to my life.”

Elana stood up with all her remaining strength. Dizzy head, foggy eyes. She took the weapon and shielded it against Death.

“Bring him back! Bring him back right now or else...”

<DeathVoice>
  “Or what? Will you use that with me? Don’t be a fool, you can’t even stand. Besides, I can’t. Even if I wanted to, there are rules. Nothing is more permanent than death.”
</DeathVoice>

She struck a blow, aiming to the head, but Death was much faster. Without a single strand of hair moving, she stopped the attack with her scissors and disarmed the girl. The <span data-action="surge">blade flew away</span> and <span class="fx-impact">pierced the mud.</span>

<DeathVoice>
  “Don’t you see? There’s nothing to be done. Even your father knew it. He went away in peace.”
</DeathVoice>

“You don’t know him. He would have fought you. He would have stayed with me. But YOU took him away.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Indeed, he fought bravely for you. For years he carried his sickness in secret. You cannot say that he just gave up.”
</DeathVoice>

Elana could not resist more, her body betrayed her. Death caught her before she hit the ground and made her land safely on the floor.

<DeathVoice>
  “I know how much you grieve; I know how much you hurt. I also lament his departure, but you have to understand that it had to happen. This anger will not help you bring him back. Everyone must go.”
</DeathVoice>

Elana opened her eyes. The slim fingers of Death wrapped her around. They were cold, but she felt warm.

“Then do me a favor and go away yourself and take this rotten world with you.”

<DeathVoice>
  “I will. It’s just a matter of time.”
</DeathVoice>

She closed her eyes in a deep sleep. Her tense body got loose. So sad, so angry.

For the first time, Death had a good look of the girl. She was beautiful even when covered by mud and tears. Elana slept in her arms; the cat purred on her legs. <span class="fx-key">Death had found the beauty of the world.</span>

Death had a long time to think. She thought of the old world and its inhabitants. She thought of the sick man and her promise. She knew well how the girl felt as she had lived through the same long ago. In her sleep, she kept having nightmares but Death sang again to calm her.

A crackling fire without firewood; a dark morning with no Sun. Elana woke up and felt the cold embrace of Death. With a quick jump, she got herself as far as she could. Her body was still tired and sore but the fear of the sight gave her strength. Death noticed that she was awake, she stood up to greet her.

“YOU... are real?” said Elana, not believing what her eyes were seeing. “I thought that the fatigue was making me imagine things.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Indeed, I’m real, more real than anything you have seen before. I am Death.”
</DeathVoice>

“Death? Does that mean you come for me?”

<DeathVoice>
  “No, not at all. I made a promise to your father and I’m here to fulfill it. You have nothing to worry about.”
</DeathVoice>

“Then, in that case, I don’t want to see you. Leave me alone! I have a place to go.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Exactly,”
</DeathVoice>

said Death as she stood up, shaking the dirt of her robe.

<DeathVoice>
  “That is the reason for my visit. I gave my word to take you to the city of gods. If you would be so kind, follow me please. We have to find some walls with a sharp corner.”
</DeathVoice>

“Can’t you hear? I told you to leave me alone. I don’t want to see you anymore. You bring bad thoughts back.”

<DeathVoice>
  “I’m afraid you don’t understand. I swore to...”
</DeathVoice>

“I don’t care what you promised. This is something I must achieve on my own. This was the wish of my father; I can take him there myself.”

Elana squeezed the locket on her neck, equipped the sword and grabbed her bag. The girl was getting away, walking not looking back. When leaving, she stepped over what seemed to be a mound of mud. Below it was her notebook, soaked and ruined. Death took it for herself, keeping it on her bag. She looked at the cat and held him on her lower set of arms. They all walked towards north, where the Sun had vanished.

<DeathVoice>
  “You know what awaits you on the way, don’t you? You know that it’s impossible to go there without my help.”
</DeathVoice>

Elana did not answer, she just continued. Death soon understood that she needed her space, so she followed behind.

<MoodShift theme="dusk" label="THE PATH OF ALMIR" />

The fields and crops were ending, they became dunes of desert. It was not as hot as one would expect, but just as dry. No water, no mud, only sand. The girl unfolded the map and calculated her location. She knew where she had gotten herself into. The infinite wastelands and in the midst of it, the path of Almir. Her father had told her about this place, this was hell even when the days were bright. Everyone who went into it never came back. The only way to cross was through the path, but the stories told that it was the home of giants, the most terrible of which was Almir, the dreadful. If she wanted to go around it, it would take months. Crossing, on the other hand was extremely difficult. She took out the compass and walked only where it marked towards. The girl took a sip of her flask, soon she would need more.

The path had a different type of sand, this was of a red color and easier to step in than the normal sand, of course it was also better compared to the mud. Even without the Sun this place had kept some heat, a pleasant surprise for Elana. She followed the reddish sand for <span class="fx-stretch">miles and miles.</span> The fields disappeared in the distance, now the landscape was completely arid.

Elana sat on the ground; she was exhausted. The dryness of the air forced her to drink some more water and stop to breathe. Death got closer and sat to wait for her a few meters away.

“Would you stop looking at me?” Elana said annoyed. “I told you that I need no help.”

The goddess remained in silence. She just looked back.

Elana sighed. “You must be either deaf or really stubborn. Either way, I don’t want your company.”

Death reached something on her bag. It was a notebook—the one Elana had thrown away a few days ago. Still covered in mud, she handed it.

“Where... did you get this,” Elana said standing up and looking closer. “I threw it away, I thought it was lost.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Nothing is lost. I knew that you would miss it in a few days. <span class="fx-key">I’m sorry if I’m meddling, but I wanted to help.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

Elana was standing in front of her, looking down at her notebook. She wanted to take it, but she was resentful of what had happened.

“Why did you do it?” Elana asked.

<DeathVoice>
  “I saw it on the ground.”
</DeathVoice>

“My father, why did you kill him?”

<DeathVoice>
  “I did not kill him. It was his time to go.”
</DeathVoice>

“No. Why do you get to decide that? Do you randomly go and take someone's life?”

<DeathVoice>
  “No, it is not arbitrary at all.”
</DeathVoice>

She took out the thing hanging on her neck.

<DeathVoice>
  “Do you see this bell over here? It tells me when a thread must be cut. It guides me.”
</DeathVoice>

“And how does the bell know? How can you be sure?”

Death did not answer. Elana didn’t know if she wanted to keep her secrets or she just didn’t know. In any way, she despised the goddess.

“You play with our lives like puppets.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Me? I do not—”
</DeathVoice>

“All the gods,” Elana clarified, interrupting her. “You use us, and then abandon us to our luck. Can you see what the world is thanks to the gods? War and plain nothingness. You should all just die with the world.”

Death remained silent again.

“You have no words to defend yourself? I don’t know why I even bother.” Elana walked away without taking her notebook. She sat close to the fire and far from the goddess.

After a few minutes, Death came closer.

<DeathVoice>
  “It is true that we have made mistakes—gods are also flawed—but I can assure you that the death of your father was not one of them.”
</DeathVoice>

“How can you be so sure?”

<DeathVoice>
  “I talked to him. He could feel it.”
</DeathVoice>

“He abandoned me.”

<DeathVoice>
  “For seven years he fought against his illness. All of that for you. You can’t say that he abandoned you. <span class="fx-key">It would be a betrayal to his memory.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

She handed the notebook back.

<DeathVoice>
  “I know that he would be happy to know that you are continuing with this.”
</DeathVoice>

Elana took the notebook with hesitation. “He loved to see me draw.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Then keep at it.”

Death said as she sat close to her, still holding Ruppert on her arms. At first, Elana tried to avoid the closeness but eventually she felt better with her presence.

“Do you enjoy it? Taking a life?”

<DeathVoice>

“Why do you keep doing it?”

“I’m sure you’ll find them too.” Elana caught herself smiling and changed her expression. She hated her but felt like she wasn’t as bad as she had thought.

“Will you be following me all the way?”

<DeathVoice>
  “I gave my word. I will protect you, even when you deny my help. Just so you know; I can take you there in the blink of an eye. You don’t need to suffer.”

“But I do. I need to do this for myself. I have nothing else on this world. I’ve been traveling for half of my life. Once I reach the place what will I do?”

<DeathVoice>
  “I understand. Still, I will offer you my help.”
</DeathVoice>

The cat woke up and stretched. He jumped down and approached Elana. He sniffed her hands and let out a curious meow.

“What is this creature?” asked the girl.

<DeathVoice>
  “You already met him a week ago. That’s a cat. I guess it is my cat.”
</DeathVoice>

*So, it was her that night.*

“Where did you find it?” Elana asked.

<DeathVoice>
  “He found me. Touch him if you want to, he likes that.”
</DeathVoice>

Elana extended her arm. At first, the cat got scared but then rubbed his face on her index finger. He smelled a bit more and then jumped on her lap. Elana petted him with both hands and the cat rubbed back on her. She smiled. He purred.

<DeathVoice>
  “It seems like he likes you.”
</DeathVoice>

“I also like him. I’ve never touched something this soft. It feels like a cloud.”

<DeathVoice>
  “He does. He got attached to me and now we travel together. Look, it’s understandable if you don’t want any help to get there, but it has become my duty to protect you. I will do so even when you don’t want me to.”
</DeathVoice>

“Then we’ll travel together.”

Elana got up and sat in front of her. She put her hand on Death’s forehead, Death replied doing the same.

“My name is Elana,” the girl said.

<DeathVoice>
  “It is my pleasure to meet you. You may call me Death.”
</DeathVoice>

After a few minutes more, they all started to walk again, but this time the path seemed shorter. They got lost in the horizon.
