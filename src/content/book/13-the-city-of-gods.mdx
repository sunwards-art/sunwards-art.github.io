Elana woke up. Ruppert atop her chest; this was a habit already. Mornings seemed brighter in that part of the world and the other side of the world rising in front of them only made it look better. Almost beautiful. Almost.

The Sun was high up but its light was not warm enough. It was chill, and dark as any other morning.

Elana could not help herself but be amazed at what she saw in front of her. She walked with her eyes locked on the horizon and her sense of wonder did not diminish with the passing hours.

*I really wish you could see this*, Elana let her mind loose. Something she hadn’t done much since the beginning of her journey. *All your life it was your dream to reach these lands. Now it’s me who got to see the wonders of the forgotten world. Not you. It should have been you.*

She felt her legs eager to run, but not to run towards the Empire. She wanted to run away, she was frightened. Her chest was hurting.

*What if there's nothing to see?* Elana thought. *What if—after more than a thousand years—everything was destroyed.* Yet she could not help but keep advancing. After a life of traveling, after all the stories of her father. She feared to find them to be false.

“Is it really that great?” Elana asked.

<DeathVoice>
  “What are you referring to, child?”
</DeathVoice>

“The Empire. The city of gods. Are all the stories true?”

<DeathVoice>
  “I’m not aware of your legends. Maybe you could refresh my mind. I can tell you what I know of to be true.”
</DeathVoice>

“They say that there’s always sunlight bathing the lands. The lands of the eternal sunshine.”

<DeathVoice>
  “It is true. The Sun shone brightly over these lands. I believe it’s just an effect of the geology. It is normal for the closer lands to be bathed by the light for longer. It shone all over the central side and never on the outer side. Just a matter of geology. But it wasn’t as good as it sounds. It messed up with their fields and animals. They also had to stay in the shadows, sunburn was common in the days of the empire.”
</DeathVoice>

*Of course the stories aren’t true*, Elana thought. *They were bedtime stories your father told you to make you sleep during the coldest nights. Don’t be silly.*

“Sunburn?” the girl inquired.

<DeathVoice>
  “It was so bright and so close that they had to be careful on how much sun they received. It burned the skin. It seemed painful.”
</DeathVoice>

“Hard to believe.” Like any of the stories.

<DeathVoice>
  “Any other legend of yours?”
</DeathVoice>

“They said that the Empire was favored by the Sun. He chose them as his people.”

<DeathVoice>
  “A bit of exaggerations. He had played favorites with humans—true—but only because they served him. The Moon did the same with her followers. The truth is that the Empire only conquered and pillaged as they wanted. In a sense, I like that they all died.”
</DeathVoice>

“Did you have any favorites?”

<DeathVoice>
  “I have you. <span class="fx-key">That is close enough.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

Odeia said with a smile.

*And I have her*, she thought. She smiled for a fraction of a second but her doubts came back. *But she is not your father. She will leave as soon as you reach the city. You’ll be alone.*

“Didn’t you have any followers like other gods did?”

<DeathVoice>
  “A few small groups, nothing notable. No churches or temples were built in my name. There were mostly sects who hid and killed in my name thinking that they would please me. They did not.”
</DeathVoice>

“They didn’t know you as I do now.”

<DeathVoice>
  “No one knew me before you. Maybe Ruppert, though it is difficult to establish conversation with him.”
</DeathVoice>

The cat was placidly sleeping on Odeia’s back.

<DeathVoice>
  “Any other question, child?”
</DeathVoice>

Elana shook her head. “I think not. I guess that all my worries will be cleared once we reach the city.”

<span class="fx-whisper">*Once you leave me behind.*</span>

The Empire was vast in extension, but mostly from west to east. It was certainly narrow towards the center and with the help of the old roads, the way would be shorter than expected.

The Sun grew bigger and, when the night was near, it started to pour again. The leak looked closer than ever, almost as if Elana could touch it. The Sun didn’t go down, instead it only seemed to go away from them. The mass of lands was diagonal to her head and it only grew in size the more they advanced.

It would take them a week before they reached the city.

*Go back*, a voice said. *You’re not worthy of these lands. You know it. Your father dedicated his life to find this place and he died on his attempt. What have you done to deserve this?*

“Shut up,” Elana said. “I won’t listen to you.”

*Is that so? Why, you are listening. You can’t stop my voice because it’s you who’s talking.*

“I don’t feel like you do.”

*But you do. You know that I speak of truths. You know that you killed your father. He died because of you.*

“That is not truth.”

*Then why do you feel so empty?*

<span class="fx-ghost">Empty.</span>

It was a morning like any other. Cold and harsh.

Far in the western lands, in a country called Millos. A small house stood with its walls almost perfect. They had to fix some issues with the roof, but the weather of the area was more lenient with those sort of things. They only had to worry about the cold.

Elana, a young girl of no more than ten years, was cooking some grains to make a soup. Her father had gone away and left her alone at noon. She was making it just the way he had showed her. He said that it was his aunt who taught them. They were survivors just like them.

But they died.

The old man reached the little house. He had a wide smile on his face. He always did.

*How can he smile so much?*

“It smells delicious. What are you preparing, my dear?”

*The same thing we’ve eaten all our lives. You don’t need to fool around. You know.*

“A soup I see. It certainly smells good.”

*No, it doesn’t. It doesn’t help if you lie.*

“I’ve come with some good news today.”

*There aren’t good news, there aren’t good days. I hate that smile of yours.*

His eyes twinkled. “Do you know what this is?”

*A map.*

“Exactly. A map. And do you know where it will take us?”

*To our doom. To your death.*

“It is an old map. I’ve been looking for it. For you, as a gift. Happy birthday my dear.”

*It is not my birthday. You couldn't possibly know. There’s no moon or stars to tell.*

“Yes today’s your birthday and as a gift we’ll go on a little adventure.”

*We traveled for seven years. We got lost. We starved. We walked until I could no more and you had to drag me. It is no ‘little adventure’.*

*Yet, why do I want to go with you? Why do I want to risk it all and travel with you again.*

*Why did you have to die.*

He stood up and coughed away from the girl.

“Don’t worry, dear. I’m fine.”

*You weren’t. You knew it and didn’t tell me. You are a liar. You always were.*

*Yet I would do anything to be lied to again.*

Elana had not woken up. Odeia threw some water over her face to no effect.

<DeathVoice>
  “The mist. She inhaled too much of it. She’s now fighting to stay in this world. How did I not notice it?”
</DeathVoice>

She stood up and made a fire. Speed was vital. She did everything she could to help her—which was not much. Holding her head in her hands, she started chanting some words. They were prayers in the tongue of gods. She knew that they wouldn’t help but she couldn’t do more.

Only hope.

Elana was too tired. The terrain was too steep, walking over rocks. Her father gave her his shoes, but it still hurt her feet. He decided to take her on his shoulders and continue.

*What are you doing? Fool. You can’t walk like that.*

“What? Don’t worry about stopping. We’ll stop when we reach the town. We must be close.”

*You aren’t. You walked for hours until we found a place to stay. You bled all the way.*

“Pain? Nah—. This old man doesn’t feel any pain.”

<span class="fx-sharp">*Lies.*</span>

“Have I ever lied to you?” She didn’t see him, she was at his back, but she knew that he was smiling.

*Yes. You always did. You’ll always do. Smiling all the way. Why can't you be honest.*

They were in a room. The room.

A sonorous voice interrupted her. "I’m thirsty, my mouth is dry. Could you go back to the cart and bring me some water?”

*More lies? You just wanted to get rid of me.*

“Worry not, my dear. I won’t go anywhere.” He was smiling.

*Even on your death bed you couldn’t tell the truth.*

“Elana,” he said with his voice failing. His face was not smiling anymore. He was serious. “I’m very proud of how much you’ve grown and I’m sure your mother would be as well.”

*I forgot this conversation. Did he say those things? I dismissed it as one of his silly comments.*

“I know how difficult things seem now, but time will make it easier.”

<span class="fx-whisper">*Time doesn’t heal scars.*</span>

“I’ve always admired how you keep pushing forward, and that’s what you… what we should do.”

*That was you. You were the one who never stopped, then you did. You stopped.*

“Always advance, one day at a time. This hope you have is what gives you strength, don’t forget it.”

*Hope? There is no hope. The world is dying. Why keep trying? Why smile at a life that just spits back at you?*

“I won’t, dad. I learned it all from you,” Elana said.

*Is that me talking? Did I promise him that?*

*I can’t. I didn’t learn everything. There were so many things you knew. You never taught me to have hope.*

*Yet I hope to see you again. Hope is foolish.*

Elana ran away. She had to be fast, her father was thirsty.

“Wait, I have to…” he mumbled out of breath. “I need to tell you something.”

*What is this? A last lie?*

“I love you, darling. I’m sorry.” He smiled with a tear on his cheek.

*No. He always told me lies but <span class="fx-key">there was no man more honest than him.</span>*

*He left a safe house for the promise of something better. He stepped over rocks, he did not eat, he gave me his blanket and clothes. He preferred to die rather than see me cold or hungry.*

*In the end he did not lie.*

“He loved you,” Elana said. “You know he did.”

*I know.*

“You aren’t actually angry at him. You are angry at yourself.”

*I am.*

“It wasn’t your fault,” said the girl. “He wouldn’t want you to believe that.”

*How can you be so sure?*

“Because I’m you, Elana. <span class="fx-key">And I have hope.</span>”

*I believe in him. He taught us well.*

*I also have hope.*

Elana took a deep breath. She was back. How much time had passed? She couldn’t know, but she felt her body weaker.

“What happened?” she shouted.

<DeathVoice>
  “It is all my fault. I’m sorry. I let you inhale the mist and it messed with your mind. I could have lost you... It’s all my fault.”
</DeathVoice>

“Don’t worry,” Elana said raising her hand to Odeia’s face. “It wasn’t your fault. It was something I had to do for myself. I became better from it.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Are you feeling better?”
</DeathVoice>

“As well as I could be,” she said with a smile. “Now, where are we? I don’t remember much.”

<DeathVoice>
  “I believe you were walking while your mind wandered by its own, my lady. We are here— <span class="fx-key">We have reached the city of gods.</span>”
</DeathVoice>

Elana paled. She <span data-action="rise">jumped and looked at the skies</span>.

Over her stood the other side of the world. Completely perpendicular at them. She saw the Sun, bigger and warmer than ever. She gave a good look at the city that stood below them.

<span class="fx-stretch">Majestic. Gold. Colossal.</span> No words were enough for her. It was not only what she expected but even more. The architecture, the columns, the roads. It was all as his father had told her.

*Hope*, she thought and smiled at herself. <span class="fx-key">*You were always right, old man.*</span>
