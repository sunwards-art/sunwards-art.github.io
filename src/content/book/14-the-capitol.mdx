Transversal to the world, they were in the middle of it. Half of Atorus stood over their heads. With good enough sight—which Odeia had plenty of—mountains, valleys, and even remains of old kingdoms were visible. It was blue and magnificent, covering most of the sky.

Below, an enormous wall rounded the perimeter, with bridges connecting it to the center, separating the city in perimeters. High towers loomed over the tall buildings, everything decorated with intricate designs. It was a <span class="fx-stretch">complex net of bridges, overpasses and viaducts,</span> going in all directions. Gold prevailed on the rooftops, spears and holy symbols shined with sunlight. Old flags and banners hung on the streets; they had been teared apart with time. They reached the City of Gods, the most important place of the Empire, the home of the Sun.

It shined over them—barely burning—leaking over the bigger castle in the center, it was the Capitol.

“We made it,” Elana said. “I can’t believe we got here.”

*We did*, Odeia thought.

She stepped forward. After eons of solitude, she could meet her creator again. She was expecting to see her father with many questions to ask.

The girl ran off down the stairs. The city was over the tallest mountain of the continent, but once at the top you had to descend, it was inside a crater. Odeia followed behind, taking Ruppert on her arms. The steps were white, made of marble, soft to the touch and bright to the sight. She felt good to have a change in the texture of the ground. She didn’t experience any pain; however, she just had her preferences—marble was better.

The gates were high, stone arches, with its doors laying on the floor. They were broken as if someone had <span class="fx-impact">knocked them down</span> and its emblem had been cut in half. More bodies on the floor. They should have expected it, but the picture still was surprising. Piles of bodies, more than they had seen before. Some of them carbonized like the others, some just slayed.

Swords and shields nailed to the ground, faces of agony. The splendid architecture was tainted by the death they had witnessed.

“More greedy men,” Elana asked.

<DeathVoice>
  “To be completely honest, I can’t tell anymore.”
</DeathVoice>

“Why else would they go to such an extent?”

Odeia didn’t respond. She didn’t know, but she also didn’t believe that greed was enough to compel entire countries to march against the Empire. There had to be a reason.

They pushed through the corpses at the gate, watching their steps. The walls had fallen in certain places, the rocks blocked parts of their way. Weapons discarded, fires still burning, and more bodies. They reached the main street, but it was fortified to defend themselves against the attack. They had to take another way to the castle.

“We can go that way,” the girl said pointing at an overpass.

They moved on, over the stairs and through the bridges. There were few places where they didn’t find soldiers, either from the Empire or the attackers. Odeia had never seen such numbers, it was a tragedy.

*Why?* she asked herself. *Why would they defy their god? What did the Sun do to gain their hatred?*

*Why does the sun bleed over this place?*

<MoodShift theme="ash" label="THE SLAIN SUN" />

She could finally see it. The corpse of the Sun floating over them, <span class="fx-key">the leak that always appeared at sunset and sunrise was there, it was his blood.</span> He had many swords on his chest, piercing his body. <span class="fx-sharp">The Sun was slayed.</span>

<span class="fx-key">*Everyone must die*, she thought. *Even the gods. We’re coming to our end.*</span>

They took a bridge that led to one of the connections of the wall. It ran all the way to the center, to the Capitol, the place where the Sun was now pouring his blood over.

<DeathVoice>
  “Is this what you expected?”
</DeathVoice>

“It is,” Elana said. “In a twisted and dark way. I still want to see what’s inside the Capitol.”

<DeathVoice>
  “What else is there to find? You have seen the world coming here, you have lost your father in the way, what would make it all worth it?”
</DeathVoice>

“There’s never a clear destination, I guess. If there’s nothing there, I will continue traveling, exploring the world until my body fails.”

<DeathVoice>
  “Why?”
</DeathVoice>

“<span class="fx-key">Life is not about a goal, it is about the little things.</span> My father wanted me to see that. It doesn’t matter how hard it is, you can still find things to live for. <span class="fx-key">The world is beautiful if you know how to look at it.</span>”

Odeia felt her words sincere. *The little things*, she thought.

“When my father died,” Elana continued, “I didn’t know how to keep going, but then you came. I have experienced many things since we’re together. In a way, I enjoyed our little journey. Those are the little things.”

<DeathVoice>
  “I know what you mean— But I have no purpose anymore. I lost it all.”
</DeathVoice>

“But you’ve gained other things. <span class="fx-key">‘We have all lost,’ you said.</span>”

It was true. If this girl who has lost everything, who has endured the dying world, and now faced it alone, still had hope, then maybe she could find her way.

<span class="fx-whisper">Maybe.</span>

They kept going through the overpass until they found themselves at the castle. Doors torn apart, blood on the walls, everything they had seen already. They weren’t surprised to find more corpses trying to besiege the place.

*Sad. This is sad. All those people who died for a foolish wish.*

They entered the main hall, the blood of the Sun came down on the center of it, lighting the tall walls. Colored windows in all directions, golden symbols and statues all around, even the floor was made of gold.

In the center stood a dome—where the emperor’s throne should be—made with some kind of metal. The leak poured inside but didn’t come out. The Capitol was gigantic and so was the dome, only its diameter was bigger than the Cathedral Odeia had destroyed weeks ago.

“A circle?” Elana asked.

<DeathVoice>
  “A sphere. Half of it, at least.”
</DeathVoice>

“Have you been here before?”

No, she hadn’t. For some reason no one ever died in that city so she had never seen the place. She attributed it to the gods' power but she wasn’t sure exactly why that happened.

<DeathVoice>
  “No, this is my first time here as well.”
</DeathVoice>

“What do you think is inside the half-sphere?”

Odeia <span class="fx-impact">knocked on the surface.</span> It was hard metal—thick by the sound of it—but not solid. The echo let them know that it was hollow.

“Let’s find the entrance,” Elana said as she ran around the dome. She took a few minutes to give it a complete loop. “There’s nothing. Not on the sides, at least.”

They turned towards the pouring liquid at the top. Elana pointed at it.

<DeathVoice>
  “Hang tight,”
</DeathVoice>

Odeia said, taking her to the top. She could climb nimbly, even when there was nothing to grip from. Once they reached the top, Elana got down.

There was an opening, covered by metal bars that let the liquid go through.

“We need to get in there,” Elana said.

<DeathVoice>
  “Do we?”
</DeathVoice>

“YES. Do you really think we can go back now and not know what’s inside there?”

<span class="fx-whisper">*Fair enough.*</span>
